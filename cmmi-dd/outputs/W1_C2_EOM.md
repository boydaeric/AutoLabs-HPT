## 1. Who can participate (A1)

### Takeaway
Only a Medicare-enrolled oncology physician group practice (PGP), identified by a single TIN and with at least one Hematology/Oncology or Medical Oncology physician, can be an EOM participant. CMS states that "Entities other than PGPs are … ineligible," so an IPA, an ACO or a clinically integrated network cannot be a participant itself [C2][H].

Member practices would each have to apply under their own TIN. EOM participants can pool only after each one is itself a participant. Being in an ACO does not make an oncology practice an EOM participant, though overlap between the two is permitted.

### Cited Findings

**Participant definition (established; cohort 1 from Jul 1, 2023, cohort 2 from Jul 1, 2025)**
- An applicant "must be a Medicare-enrolled oncology PGP identifiable by a unique federal taxpayer identification number (TIN)." It must have at least one EOM practitioner, and at least one practitioner must be an "oncology practitioner" (specialty code Hematology/Oncology or Medical Oncology). The PGP must be a legal entity authorized to do business in each state where it operates. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §II.B.i](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [2022 RFA §II.B](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://innovation.cms.gov/media/document/eom-rfa); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)
- The FAQ says it outright: "Entities other than PGPs are also ineligible to participate in EOM." Critical Access Hospitals, FQHCs and RHCs are excluded. PGPs that "routinely refer beneficiaries to PPS-Exempt Cancer Hospitals (PCHs) for chemotherapy services are not eligible." [C2][H] — [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions); [2024 RFA §II.B.i](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- The application template describes the applicant as "a Medicare-enrolled oncology [PGP] identified by a single [TIN]." The legal entity named "must be the same legal entity that would execute a participation agreement." Applicants must list every TIN used for oncology billing since July 1, 2016, including TINs that came in through mergers and acquisitions. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA App. E](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- The template's organizational-structure checklist includes "Hospital Based," "Multi-state Health Care System" and "Organization Affiliated." So a health-system-affiliated PGP can apply, as long as it applies as a single-TIN PGP. [C2][M] — [2024 RFA App. E](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- Current participants include several health-system practice groups:
  - Prisma Health University Medical Group
  - the UNC Faculty Practice
  - Trinity Health IHA Medical Group Hematology Oncology
  - TriHealth H, LLC
  - Vidant Medical Group
  - a cancer center at Cabell Huntington Hospital
  - "Bozeman Health Deaconess Hospital"
  [C2][H] — [EOM Participant List XLSX](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-participants)

**EOM practitioners (established)**
- An EOM practitioner is a Medicare-enrolled physician or non-physician practitioner (NPI) who:
  - furnishes E&M services for an included cancer type;
  - bills under the PGP's TIN;
  - has reassigned billing rights to the PGP; and
  - appears on the PGP's EOM Practitioner List.
  [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §II.B.ii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- CMS slides say the Practitioner List is "to be updated semiannually." [C2][M] — [Cohort 2 Office Hour #2 slides (Aug 29, 2024)](https://www.cms.gov/eom-cohort2-app-spt-oh2-slides)
- CMS removes individuals from the list only for non-compliance or program-integrity reasons. [C2][M] — [2024 RFA §VI.C](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Must every oncologist billing under the TIN participate? Not stated in public documents**
- Episode attribution is TIN-level. An episode goes to the TIN that billed the first qualifying E&M, provided that TIN billed at least 25% of qualifying E&Ms; otherwise it goes to the TIN with the plurality. A TIN is eligible for attribution if any Hem/Onc or Medical Oncology practitioner billed a qualifying E&M under it. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.A.viii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)
- CMS asks for the "proposed" EOM practitioners. No public document says every oncologist under the TIN must be listed. [C2][M] — [2024 RFA App. E](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Pooling (established)**
- Two or more EOM participants may form a voluntary pool. CMS then computes a single benchmark, a single performance-based payment (PBP) or recoupment (PBR), and a single quality score for the pool. One "pooled payee" receives or pays on behalf of the pool. Pool members must pick a single risk arrangement. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §II.B.iii, §V.C.iii.6, §V.F](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)
- Any PGP an applicant wants to pool with "must also submit an [EOM PGP] Application (if not currently participating in EOM)." [C2][H] — [2024 RFA App. E](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- Mandatory pooling applies when a pair of PGPs exceeds 20% "billing overlap": shared practitioners billed more than 20% of all qualifying E&Ms for the pair's reference-period episodes.
  - The participant must then form a pool with the other PGP, reduce the overlap, or terminate.
  - A non-EOM PGP can join a mandatory pool only if it applies, is offered a Participation Agreement, and executes it before the performance period starts. It must pass the same eligibility and program-integrity screening as any other applicant.
  - Determinations are made each performance period, using a reference period about two years earlier (PP10 uses Jan–Jun 2026; PP13 uses Jul–Dec 2027).
  [C2][H] — [Payment Methodology v7.0, App. J](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology); [2024 RFA §II.B.iii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Care Partners (established)**
- A Care Partner is any Medicare-enrolled provider or supplier that performs at least one participant redesign activity (PRA), has a Care Partner arrangement with a participant, appears on the participant's Care Partner list, and is not an EOM practitioner. Financial arrangements with Care Partners are allowed. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §II.B.iv, §VII.B.i](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)
- **Contradiction on list cadence.** The RFA body says the Care Partner list must be resubmitted "on at least a semiannual basis." The application template says CMS will collect lists "on at least an annual basis." [C2][M] — [2024 RFA §II.B.iv vs. App. E](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Payer participants (established)**
- EOM is multi-payer: private plans, Medicare Advantage plans, state Medicaid agencies and Medicaid MCOs may sign a memorandum of understanding (MOU) as "EOM Payers."
  - A payer must partner with at least one EOM participant.
  - Unlike practices, payers "may apply … at any time during the model performance period."
  - CMS does not set the amount or form of payer payments.
  [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §II.B, §II.B.vi](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)
- The only current EOM payer is BlueCross BlueShield of South Carolina. [C2][H] — [CMS EOM page (mod. 07/31/2026)](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/enhancing-oncology-model)

### Path tests

**(a) The Client's oncology practice(s) apply directly.**
- Permitted in principle only if each practice is a single-TIN, Medicare-enrolled oncology PGP with a Hem/Onc or Medical Oncology physician [C2][H].
- In practice this path is closed now, because no application window is open (see §2).
- An IPA-level application is prohibited ("Entities other than PGPs are … ineligible") [C2][H].

**(b) The practice(s) join through, or are covered by, an existing participant or ACO.**
- *Reassigning billing to an existing participant.* Becoming an EOM practitioner of an existing participant by reassigning billing to its TIN and being added to its Practitioner List (updated semiannually) fits the definitions [C2][M]. No public document addresses acquisitions mid-model; this is unaddressed.
- *No participant to join in New York.* No current participant is based in New York; the nearest is in Connecticut [C2][H].
- *ACO membership.* Being in an MSSP or REACH ACO does not confer EOM participation. Overlap is permitted (§6) [C2][H].
- *Care Partner route.* The health system, or another Medicare-enrolled entity, could be a Care Partner of an EOM participant. It would not be a participant and would carry no PBP/PBR accountability [C2][M].
- *Mandatory-pool route.* A non-EOM practice with more than 20% billing overlap with an existing participant can apply to join that participant's mandatory pool before a performance period [C2][H]. This is a narrow mechanism, not a general intake window.

### Inferences
- The IPA and the affiliated health system would not be "the participant." The applying entity would have to be an oncology PGP TIN, such as a health-system medical group TIN or an independent oncology group TIN.
- Because attribution is TIN-level, episodes from oncologists billing under the TIN would likely be attributed to the participant whether or not each oncologist is on the list. Off-list oncologists would create unmanaged cost exposure. This is an inference from the attribution rules.
- The PCH exclusion matters especially in New York. Two of the PPS-exempt cancer hospitals are in New York State (Memorial Sloan Kettering and Roswell Park, from background knowledge, *not verified in this session*). A practice that routinely refers patients to them for chemotherapy would be ineligible [C2][L — needs verification].
- The "Bozeman Health Deaconess Hospital" entry seems to sit uneasily with "Entities other than PGPs are ineligible." It probably reflects a hospital-owned PGP billing under the hospital's TIN, but the documents do not explain it [C2][L].

### Gaps
- The Participation Agreement is not public. Rules on adding practitioners, TIN changes, mergers and change of control during the model, and whether all oncologists under a TIN must be listed, could not be confirmed.
- Whether CMS would accept a new PGP application in 2026–2027 for a mandatory pool is not addressed beyond the methodology text.

## 3. Episode and payment mechanics

### Takeaway
EOM uses 6-month, total-cost-of-care episodes, triggered by systemic therapy for 7 cancer types. The Monthly Enhanced Oncology Services (MEOS) payment is now $110 per beneficiary per month (PBPM), or $140 for dual eligibles, in both cohorts. The two risk arrangements are unchanged since 2022 except that the recoupment threshold moved from 98% to 100% of benchmark [C2][H]. Risk Arrangement 2 (RA2) is an Advanced APM; Risk Arrangement 1 (RA1) is a MIPS APM.

### Cited Findings

**Cancer types (established)**
- High-risk breast, chronic leukemia, small intestine/colorectal, lung, lymphoma, multiple myeloma, and high-risk/high-intensity prostate.
- Low-risk breast (long-term oral endocrine only) and low-intensity prostate (ADT and/or anti-androgen only) are excluded.
- Episodes with CAR-T, bispecific antibodies or a COVID-19 diagnosis are excluded.
[C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.A.iv, §V.A.vii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)

**Trigger and length (established)**
- An episode starts with an initiating cancer therapy: a Part B claim with an included cancer diagnosis, or a Part D fill with a Part B cancer claim on the fill date or in the prior 59 days.
- It requires a qualifying E&M (CPT 99201–99205 or 99211–99215) within 6 months.
- It lasts 6 months; a new episode starts if therapy continues.
- CMS updates the initiating-therapy list each performance period.
[C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.A.v–vi](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [Payment Methodology v7.0 §1.2.1](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)

**MEOS by cohort (established)**

| Cohort / period | Base MEOS | Dual-eligible add-on | Total for duals | Effective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cohort 1, original | $70 PBPM | +$30 | $100 | Jul 1, 2023 – Dec 31, 2024 dates of service |
| Cohort 1, updated | $110 PBPM | +$30 | $140 | Dates of service on or after Jan 1, 2025 |
| Cohort 2 | $110 PBPM | +$30 | $140 | From Jul 1, 2025 |

- The base MEOS counts toward total cost of care; the $30 dual add-on does not.
- Participants can bill up to 6 MEOS per episode, one per calendar month. The billable window runs from 30 days before to 30 days after the episode. Claims can be filed in real time or up to 12 months after service.
- Beneficiaries pay no cost-sharing on MEOS.
- There is **no HRSN-based adjustment**; dual status is the only adjustment.
[C2][H] — [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions); [2024 RFA §V.C.ii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [2022 RFA §V.C.ii](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://innovation.cms.gov/media/document/eom-rfa); [Second-Cohort Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/enhancing-oncology-model/second-cohort-fact-sheet)

**Benchmark and target (established)**
- CMS starts with predicted expenditures from cancer-type-specific price models.
- It then applies:
  - an experience adjuster, blending national, regional and participant-specific spending (national and regional only where the participant had fewer than 50 baseline episodes of that cancer type);
  - clinical adjusters for ever-metastatic status (breast, lung, colorectal) and HER2 status (breast), using data the participant submits;
  - a retrospective, cancer-type-specific trend factor computed only from non-EOM practices;
  - a novel-therapy adjustment.
- The benchmark is the sum of episode benchmark prices. The target is the benchmark minus the EOM discount.
[C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.C.iii.3](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [Payment Methodology v7.0 §4](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)

**Rebasing (established 9/14/2026, for PP8–PP13 = episodes starting Jan 1, 2027 onward)**
- Baseline for PP1–PP7: episodes initiating Jul 2016–Jun 2020.
- New baseline for PP8–PP13: episodes initiating Jan 2019–Dec 2022.
- The new baseline's initiating-therapy list "excludes most hormonal therapies," and bispecific-antibody episodes are excluded from it.
[C2][M — single document, newly issued] — [Payment Methodology v7.0 §1 and Revision History](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)

**Novel-therapy adjustment (established)**
- Applies when, for a given cancer type, a participant's share of episode spending on "new" FDA-approved oncology drugs (approved after Jun 30, 2021; "new" for about 2 years per indication) exceeds the non-EOM average, with use consistent with FDA indications.
- The adjustment equals 80% of the difference between the two shares.
- It can only raise the benchmark, never lower it.
- CMS publishes a novel therapies list; v1.36 is dated Sep 2026.
[C2][H] — [Payment Methodology v7.0 §4.2.2](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology); [Participant Resources](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/eom-participant-resources)

**Risk arrangements (established; both cohorts)**

| | Discount (target as % of benchmark) | Stop-loss | Stop-gain | Recoupment threshold | QPP status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RA1 (default) | 4% (target 96%) | 2% of benchmark | 4% of benchmark | 98% for PP1–PP3; **100% for PP4–PP13** | MIPS APM |
| RA2 | 3% (target 97%) | 6% of benchmark | 12% of benchmark | 98% for PP1–PP3; **100% for PP4–PP13** | Advanced APM and MIPS APM |

- Cohort 1 moved to the 100% threshold from PP4 (episodes starting Jan 1, 2025). Cohort 2 has had 100% from the start (Jul 1, 2025).
- Spending between the target and the threshold is a neutral zone.
- Participants can switch arrangements semiannually, before the next performance period.
- Downside risk applies from day one; there is no upside-only option.
[C2][H] — [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions); [2024 RFA §V.C.iii.6, Table 4](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [2022 RFA §V.C.iii.6](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://innovation.cms.gov/media/document/eom-rfa); [Payment Methodology v7.0 Intro](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)

**Quality adjustment (established)**
- The aggregate quality score (AQS) translates into multipliers:
  - PBP multiplier: 100% at AQS ≥75%, 75% at 50–74%, 50% at 30–49%, 0% below 30%.
  - PBR multiplier: 90% at AQS ≥75%, 95% at 50–74%, 100% below 50%.
- Quality can therefore reduce a recoupment by up to 10%.
[C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.F, Tables 7–8](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Reconciliation (established)**
- Semiannual, with an initial reconciliation after at least 1 month of claims run-out and one true-up 12 months later (13 months of run-out in total). [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.C.iii.7](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Advanced APM / MIPS status (established, from July 2023)**
- RA2 meets 42 CFR §414.1415 as an Advanced APM; RA1 does not meet the nominal-risk standard and is a MIPS APM only.
- Practitioners on the Practitioner List can use the APM Performance Pathway (APP).
- Participants must certify CEHRT use every year.
[C2][H] — [2024 RFA §VI](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [Second-Cohort Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/enhancing-oncology-model/second-cohort-fact-sheet)
- MIPS payment adjustment factors do not apply to MEOS payments. [C2][H] — [Federal Register notice, Jul 14, 2022](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/07/14/2022-15062/medicare-program-mips-payment-adjustment-exception-applicable-for-enhancing-oncology-model-monthly)

### Inferences
- With RA1's 4% discount and only 2% stop-loss, the most a participant can lose in RA1 is 2% of benchmark. MEOS revenue ($110 × up to 6 months per episode) is part of the cost base that must come in under target.

### Gaps
- Advanced APM status and QP thresholds for performance years 2026–2027 were not re-verified in the CY2026 or CY2027 Physician Fee Schedule rules or on the QPP APM list in this session.

## 4. Participant redesign activities (PRAs) and data/reporting requirements (A4)

### Takeaway
There are eight required PRAs, plus three streams of data reported to CMS:
- beneficiary-level clinical data elements (CDEs), semiannually, for at least 90% of attributed episodes;
- sociodemographic data elements (SDEs), with the CDEs;
- two aggregate MIPS-style quality measures (EOM-4 and EOM-5), annually.

Participants also make annual attestations for the PRAs and for CEHRT. Reporting can be done manually through a pre-populated Excel template, so no FHIR integration is required. However:
- ePROs must be integrated into the EHR (the strength of that requirement is contested in CMS's own documents);
- 24/7 access requires real-time record access;
- the continuous quality improvement (CQI) requirement presumes the participant can ingest and analyze monthly CMS claims files.
[C2][H]

### Cited Findings: the eight PRAs (established for both cohorts; first five Enhanced Services due within 90 days of start)

1. **24/7 access** to "an appropriate clinician who has real-time access to the EOM participant's medical records." *Implication:* every site and after-hours clinician needs real-time EHR access; with multiple EHRs that means cross-system access. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)
2. **Patient navigation**, available to all eligible beneficiaries who request or need it, meeting minimum standards in RFA Appendix C. *Implication:* a workflow and documentation task; no data reporting to CMS. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B.i](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
3. **Care plan** with the 13 Institute of Medicine (IOM) Care Management Plan components (RFA Appendix D). *Implication:* documentation in the EHR; auditable; not submitted to CMS. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
4. **Treatment consistent with nationally recognized guidelines.** [C2][H] — same.
5. **HRSN screening**, at minimum for food insecurity, transportation and housing instability, using any tool (examples: AHC, PRAPARE, NCCN Distress Thermometer).
   - The guide says participants attest annually through the PRA Attestation.
   - CMS "may require that EOM participants report HRSN data to CMS, beginning in later performance periods" (proposed, no date).
   - *Implication:* capture in the EHR or ePRO tool; no current submission to CMS.
   [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B.i.5](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [HRSN Guide v2.4 (Nov 25, 2025)](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-health-related-social-needs-guide)
6. **ePROs**, covered in detail below.
7. **Use data for CQI.** "Using data includes the collection and reporting of certain data to CMS, and utilizing data provided by CMS … (e.g., dashboards and feedback and reconciliation reports)." *Implication:* this is where the lack of an aggregation layer bites (see the CMS data list below). [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B.ii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
8. **CEHRT** as defined in 42 CFR §414.1305, used in a manner sufficient for §414.1415(a)(1)(i), with annual certification. *Implication:* Epic certified-module status should satisfy this; point solutions do not need certification unless they are relied on for CEHRT functions. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B.iii, §VI.A](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

### Cited Findings: ePROs (established; timing differs by cohort)

**Tool and domains**
- No specific tool is required, but it must cover four domains: symptoms/toxicities, functioning, behavioral health, and HRSN.
- Collection must be electronic (portal, interactive voice response, SMS, or a tablet in the waiting room).
- ePROs must be collected before each visit with a qualifying E&M, except the first visit, and reviewed with the patient at that visit.
[C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B.i.6](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [ePRO Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-epros-fs)

**EHR integration: contradiction surfaced**
- The 2024 RFA says participants "will be required to integrate ePROs data into electronic health records."
- The ePRO Fact Sheet says "Model participants must integrate ePRO data into electronic health records."
- But ePRO Guide v2.5 (May 22, 2026) is softer: participants "must collect ePROs data and integrate it into their information system workflow. *Ideally*, this will include some level of integration with electronic medical records."
[C2][M — CMS documents disagree] — [2024 RFA §V.B.i.6.b](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [ePRO Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-epros-fs); [ePRO Guide v2.5](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-elec-pat-rpt-outcomes)

**Reporting to CMS**
- ePRO data are **not** reported to CMS currently. CMS "may require" reporting "in later performance periods" (proposed). [C2][H] — same sources.

**Phase-in**
- Cohort 1: model years 1–2 (PP1–PP4, Jul 2023–Jun 2025) optional; required from **PP5 (Jul 1, 2025)**. [C2][H] — [2022 RFA §V.B.i.6.d](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://innovation.cms.gov/media/document/eom-rfa); [ePRO Guide v2.5](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-elec-pat-rpt-outcomes)
- Cohort 2: PP5–PP8 optional; required from **PP9 (Jul 1, 2027)**. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.B.i.6.d](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [ePRO Guide v2.5](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-elec-pat-rpt-outcomes)
- **Contradiction on example percentages.** The 2022 and 2024 RFAs say "e.g., 35%, 50%, 70%"; ePRO Guide v2.5 and the Fact Sheet say "35 percent, 50 percent, 75 percent." CMS calls all of these examples, so the binding percentages are unstated publicly (proposed). [C2][M]

### Cited Findings: data submitted to CMS (established)

**Three data types, submitted through the CMS Innovation Support Platform's Health Data Reporting (HDR) application**
- Quality measure data, beneficiary-level clinical data, and beneficiary-level sociodemographic data, reported "no more than once per performance period." [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §VIII.A](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)

**Clinical data elements (CDEs)**
- Due semiannually, within 30 days of attribution data being made available, for at least 90% of attributed episodes.
- Cohort 1 began with PP1 (initial reporting Fall 2024). Cohort 2 begins with PP5 (initial reporting Fall 2026, true-up Fall 2027).
- Elements include:
  - beneficiary identifiers;
  - attributed cancer type and episode dates;
  - ICD-10 code and initial diagnosis date;
  - clinical status, recurrence/relapse and trend;
  - history of metastatic cancer;
  - TNM staging;
  - ER, PR and HER2 results;
  - histology.
- The data are used for payment (the ever-metastatic and HER2 adjusters) as well as for monitoring.
[C2][H] — [CDE Guide v2.5 (May 22, 2026)](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-clinical-data-elements-guide); [2024 RFA §V.F](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- Submission format:
  - "low-tech": the pre-populated HDR Excel template, which is the only accepted template format; or
  - "high-tech": an HL7 FHIR API following the EOM Implementation Guide, aligned to mCODE.
  - SDE-only submissions must use the template.
  [C2][H] — [CDE Guide v2.5 §3](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-clinical-data-elements-guide)
- *Implication:* staging and biomarker data usually sit in oncology or pathology modules (for example, Epic Beacon) or outside documents. Without an aggregation layer this means manual abstraction for each attributed episode.

**Sociodemographic data elements (SDEs)**
- The RFA lists race, ethnicity, preferred language, sex assigned at birth, gender identity, sexual orientation and disability status, reported "if available." Beneficiaries may decline. [C2][M] — [2024 RFA §VIII.A](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- **Possible change (secondary source only).** AJMC (Apr 22, 2025) reports that a "2025 unilateral amendment" to EOM agreements changed SDOH and sociodemographic data requirements to comply with EO 14151 and 14168. It also reports CMS "will not require or accept submissions of health equity plans in EOM for 2025 and beyond," while HRSN screening was retained. [C2][L] — [AJMC](https://ajmc.com/view/while-claiming-transparency-cms-quietly-drops-health-equity-elements-of-eom)
- That account contradicts the 2024 RFA's health equity plan requirement (§V.B.ii). The SDE Guide v2.6 (not read) is still posted.

**Quality measures (established)**
- EOM-1, EOM-2 and EOM-3 are claims-based:
  - EOM-1: admissions and ED visits after chemotherapy;
  - EOM-2: hospice admission of 3 days or more before death;
  - EOM-3: chemotherapy in the last 14 days of life.
- EOM-4 (pain assessment and plan of care) and EOM-5 (depression screening and follow-up) are participant-reported:
  - submitted annually as aggregate results under MIPS CQM specifications, **for all qualifying patients under the PGP TIN**, not only EOM beneficiaries;
  - submission window January to mid-February; cohort 1 started in 2025, cohort 2 starts in early 2027.
- EOM-6 is a patient-experience survey run by a CMS contractor.
- The maximum is 69 points from PP4 onward; EOM-1 was cut from 12 to 9 points effective PP4.
[C2][H] — [Payment Methodology v7.0 §7](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology); [Quality Scoring Update (Nov 25, 2024)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/eom-qual-paymnt-updates.pdf)
- *Implication:* TIN-wide CQM reporting requires one denominator across every site and system under the TIN.

**Attestations**
- Annual PRA attestation (including HRSN) and annual CEHRT certification. [C2][H] — [HRSN Guide v2.4](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-health-related-social-needs-guide); [2024 RFA §VI.A](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Data CMS provides (on request, after signing a Data Request and Attestation)**
- Quarterly feedback reports and dashboards.
- Semiannual reconciliation and attribution reports.
- Semiannual MEOS recoupment reports.
- Monthly beneficiary-identifiable line-level claims.
[C2][H] — [2024 RFA §VIII.B](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- *Implication:* using these for CQI and for real-time episode management requires a claims-ingestion and analytics capability.

**PBP eligibility depends on data compliance**
- To be eligible for a PBP, a participant must submit "accurately, completely, and timely" all required data elements and implement the PRAs. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.C.iii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)

**Burden evidence**
- At-a-Glance: practices used CMS episode data "supplemented with their own data sources." [C2][M] — [Evaluation Exec Summary (Aug 2025)](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/data-and-reports/2025/eom-1st-eval-report-exec-sum)
- Trade press (secondary) reports participants citing clinical-data and SDOH reporting burden. [C2][L] — [Oncology News Central (2025; page returned 403, seen only through a search snippet)](https://www.oncologynewscentral.com/oncology/cancer-practices-still-concerned-about-cms-enhancing-oncology-model)

### Inferences
- An organization with Epic and no aggregation layer could meet the CDE and SDE obligations through the Excel template by manual abstraction. Ongoing cost would scale with attributed episodes (participants' median was about 255 episodes per 6-month period).
- The harder dependencies are:
  - ePRO-to-EHR integration, if the ePRO is a point solution;
  - real-time 24/7 record access across systems;
  - TIN-wide CQM aggregation for EOM-4 and EOM-5;
  - ingesting CMS monthly claims to manage total cost of care, including Part D drug spend.

### Gaps
- The exact cohort 2 ePRO percentages, and whether ePRO, HRSN or SDE data reporting will become mandatory before 2030.
- The current SDE list after the 2025 amendment, which is confirmed only by a secondary source.

## 5. Volume and case-count thresholds (A5)

### Takeaway
**No minimum number of episodes or beneficiaries is required to apply or participate**, and there is no low-volume exemption or rule [C2][H for absence in the RFAs, FAQ and methodology]. Volume matters only through:
- quality-measure minimum denominators;
- the experience-adjuster blend;
- the CDE 90% reporting floor.

Voluntary pooling is the documented way to aggregate volume across participants.

### Cited Findings
- The eligibility criteria have no volume floor: one EOM practitioner, including one oncology practitioner. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §II.B](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)
- **Quality minimum denominators** (established; measures below the minimum are handled under PM §7.3):
  - EOM-1: 50 episodes across two performance periods;
  - EOM-2 and EOM-3: 20 episodes across two performance periods;
  - EOM-4a, EOM-4b and EOM-5: 20 encounters or patients per calendar year.
  [C2][H] — [Payment Methodology v7.0 §7.2](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)
- **Experience adjuster**: below 50 attributed baseline episodes of a cancer type, only national and regional ratios are used for that type, with no participant-specific component. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.C.iii.3(b)](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [Payment Methodology v7.0](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)
- **CDE reporting** must cover at least 90% of attributed episodes. [C2][H] — [CDE Guide v2.5](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-clinical-data-elements-guide)
- **Pooling**: voluntary pools aggregate benchmark, spending and quality across participants. Mandatory pools are triggered at more than 20% billing overlap. [C2][H] — [Payment Methodology v7.0 App. J](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)
- **Observed volumes** (PP1 true-up, 42 participant rows): smallest participant 21 episodes, median about 258, largest 5,110 per 6-month period. PP3 initial: 27 / 255 / 4,830. [C2][H] — [EOM Cost & Quality Data File PP1–PP3](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/eom-cost-qual-pp1-2.xlsx)
- Participants had "nearly 3 times the number of oncology practitioners and almost 4 times the episode volume of nonparticipating practices." [C2][H] — [Evaluation At-a-Glance (Aug 2025)](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/data-and-reports/2025/eom-1st-eval-report-aag)

### Inferences
- A practice with low episode counts can participate. However, its spending results will be volatile (6-month total-cost-of-care with 5th–95th percentile winsorization), and its quality measures may fall below the scoring minimums. In practice, that pushes small practices toward pooling or RA1.

### Gaps
- PM §7.3 (how measures below the minimum denominator are scored) was not extracted in detail.

## 6. Interactions with other models

### Takeaway
Overlap with MSSP, ACO REACH, KCC, ACO PC Flex, BPCI Advanced, CJR, TEAM, PCF/MCP, GUIDE, Maryland TCOC, AHEAD, IBH and IOTA is permitted [C2][H].
- **ACO overlap**: CMS generally claws back the part of an EOM PBP that duplicates shared savings paid to an ACO containing the same TIN, **except for MSSP**, where no clawback applies.
- **Episode-model overlap (BPCI-A, CJR, TEAM)**: savings or losses accrue first to the other model's episode, and the prorated reconciliation amount is then counted in EOM spending.
- **LEAD** (starting Jan 1, 2027): not yet addressed in EOM documents. The LEAD RFA says overlap is "likely" permitted.

### Cited Findings
- **ACO initiatives (established, PP1 onward):**
  - EOM spending is adjusted to what FFS would have paid without population-based payments, and the ACO's capitation for overlapping beneficiaries is excluded.
  - Non-claims ACO payments and recoupments are excluded.
  - "If a portion of the EOM discount is paid out as shared savings to an ACO … that includes a health care provider or supplier who bills under the same TIN as an EOM participant … CMS will generally recover that portion of the PBP."
  - "However, if the ACO initiative uses retrospective growth rates … such as in the case of the Shared Savings Program, CMS will not recover the overlapping EOM PBP."
  [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.E.ii.1](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [Payment Methodology v7.0 §5.2, §6.3.1](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)
- **ACO PC Flex (established, PP4 onward):** handled like REACH and MSSP. Prospective primary care payments are replaced by standardized FFS amounts, and Advanced Shared Savings Payments are excluded. **IOTA (PP4 onward):** overlap permitted; IOTA PBP/PBR excluded. [C2][H] — [EOM PM Updates effective Jan 1, 2025 (Nov 1, 2024)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/eom-payment-method-updates-jan-2025.pdf)
- **BPCI Advanced, CJR and TEAM (TEAM from Jan 1, 2026):** "any reductions or increases in expenditures will first accrue to the BPCI Advanced or CJR Model episode," and likewise for TEAM. The prorated reconciliation amount is then included in EOM episode spending. [C2][H] — [2024 RFA §V.E.ii.2](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024); [Payment Methodology v7.0 §5.2 (TEAM added in v6.0, 11/3/2025)](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)
- **AHEAD, IBH, PCF/MDPCP, MCP, GUIDE:** overlap permitted. Global budgets and prospective payments are replaced with standardized FFS amounts; infrastructure and cooperative-agreement funds are excluded. [C2][H] — [Payment Methodology v7.0 §5.2](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology); [2024 RFA §V.E.ii](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)
- **LEAD (announced/proposed):** "Participant TINs will likely be permitted to simultaneously participate in LEAD and in the GUIDE, TEAM, ACCESS, ASM, and EOM Models." Overlap policies are to be published in annual LEAD methodology papers. LEAD runs Jan 1, 2027–Dec 31, 2036. [C2][M] — [LEAD RFA](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/lead-rfa.pdf); [LEAD page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/lead)
- EOM Payment Methodology v7.0 (Sep 2026) does not mention LEAD. [C2][H]

### Inferences
- If the Client's health system or IPA runs an MSSP ACO that includes an EOM participant's TIN, the participant would keep its EOM PBP under current policy, and the ACO's savings would reflect any reduced spending.
- Under REACH, or potentially LEAD (treatment not yet published), CMS may claw back duplicative PBP.

### Gaps
- How EOM–LEAD overlap will be handled financially.
- The REACH-to-LEAD transition for 2027 as it affects EOM.

## Implications for the decision anchor

**What the evidence permits.**
- **Direct participation by the IPA, its ACO or a CIN is prohibited.** Only a single-TIN, Medicare-enrolled oncology PGP can be a participant [C2][H].
- **Direct entry by the Client's oncology practice(s) is effectively closed.** No practice application window is open after the Sep 16, 2024 cohort 2 deadline, and none was announced through Oct 8, 2026. The model ends Jun 30, 2030 [C2][H].
- **Joining through an existing participant is permitted only in narrow forms:**
  - the Client's oncologists reassign billing to a participant's TIN and are added to its semiannually updated Practitioner List (rules for acquisitions and mid-model additions are not public);
  - a Client entity serves as a Care Partner, with no EOM accountability;
  - a mandatory-pool admission if a Client practice had more than 20% billing overlap with a participant.
- **That route is geographically thin.** No participant is in New York [C2][H].
- **The Client's overlap and burden picture.** Any MSSP/REACH ACO the Client runs can co-exist with EOM participants, and in MSSP the EOM PBP is not clawed back [C2][H]. The data burden is achievable by manual abstraction, but it presumes ePRO–EHR integration, real-time cross-site record access, TIN-wide CQM aggregation and claims analytics, which the Client currently lacks [C2][H/M].
- **The decision set the evidence leaves open.** Deferring or declining EOM as a direct option, and treating any "join through" option as contingent on a specific existing participant's willingness and on unpublished Participation Agreement terms. This is a statement of what the evidence permits, not a recommendation.
