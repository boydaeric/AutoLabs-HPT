# C3 — CJR-X (Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement Expanded) Model: primary-source brief (current to 8 Oct 2026)

**Scope:** Wave 1, card C3. Tests A3 (is the Client's hospital required or exempt, and how can the IPA's physicians share in results), A4 (data and reporting burden), and A5 (volume thresholds). Decision anchor: how the Client should prepare and position for a mandatory model.

**How to read the tags**
- **Status labels:**
  - **[Established]** means codified in the final rule (CMS-1849-F), with its effective date.
  - **[Proposed]** means it appeared in CMS-1849-P only.
  - **[Announced]** means CMS web or fact-sheet material that is not in regulation.
- **Confidence grades:**
  - **H** means the codified regulatory text agrees with at least one other independent source (CMS model page, participant file, OACT memo, evaluation report, or trade summaries).
  - **M** means a single strong source, usually the final-rule text or preamble alone.
  - **L** means thin, ambiguous or contested evidence.
- **Pin cites:** "Reg. §512.6xx" means 42 CFR part 512, subpart F, as added by the final rule at 91 FR 50349–50367. "Pmbl." means the final-rule preamble, section X.C, at 91 FR 50109–50253.
- **Method:** I read and searched the full GPO text of both the final and proposed rules locally. The proposed-to-final comparison comes from a line-level diff of the subpart F regulatory text in each rule.

## 1. Status and timeline

### Takeaway
CJR-X is final, codified regulation, not a proposal. CMS issued the rule on July 31, 2026, and it was published in the Federal Register on August 4, 2026 at 91 FR 49570–50461 (FR Doc. 2026-15833). Episodes begin January 1, 2028, and performance years follow the calendar year. PY1 uses a CY2024–CY2026 baseline. CMS posted a participant list early, with data as of September 30, 2026. Preliminary target prices have not been issued and are due "prior to the start of" PY1. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **Final rule citation [Established] [C3] H.**
  - Medicare Program; Hospital IPPS and LTCH PPS … FY 2027 Rates (CMS-1849-F and CMS-0062-F), FR Doc. 2026-15833, **91 FR 49570–50461, published Tuesday, August 4, 2026**. The rules section takes effect October 1, 2026 ([FR final rule][FR-F]; [GPO text][GPO-F]).
  - CJR-X itself applies only to episodes starting on or after **January 1, 2028** (Reg. §512.630(a), 91 FR 50355).
- **The Jul 31 vs Aug 4 conflict is resolved [C3] H.** CMS's fact sheet says CMS *issued* the final rule on **July 31, 2026** ([CMS fact sheet][CMS-FS]). The Federal Register *published* it on **August 4, 2026** ([FR][FR-F]). Both dates are correct: July 31 is the release and public-inspection date; August 4 is the official publication date. For legal citation, use 91 FR 49570 (Aug. 4, 2026).
- **Proposed rule [Established as historical fact] [C3] H.**
  - CMS-1849-P, FR Doc. 2026-07203, **91 FR 19312–19887, published April 14, 2026** ([FR proposed rule][FR-P]; [FR API metadata][FR-P-API]).
  - **Anomaly (L):** the proposed rule's DATES paragraph says comments were due "no later than 5 p.m. EDT on **April 10, 2026**", which is four days before publication ([GPO proposed text][GPO-P]). This looks like an FR typographical error. I could not confirm the actual comment deadline.
- **What changed from proposed to final.** Each pair below is proposed → final; all final items are [Established], from the final-rule text and the regulatory-text diff.
  - **Start date:** Oct 1, 2027 (FY-aligned PYs) → **Jan 1, 2028, calendar-year PYs**. CMS called this a "targeted three-month implementation delay" giving about 17 months between publication and start (Pmbl. X.C.2.a, 91 FR 50125–50128; Reg. §512.605 "performance year", "model start date"). [C3] H. Secondary sources agree ([Fierce Healthcare][FIERCE]; [HIT Consultant][HITC]).
  - **PY1 baseline:** episodes Oct 1, 2023 – Sept 30, 2026 → **Jan 1, 2024 – Dec 31, 2026** (Reg. §512.640(b)(2)(i)). [C3] M
  - **TEAM-to-CJR-X handoff:** under the proposal, TEAM hospitals would have waited about 9 months, joining on Oct 1, 2031. Under the final rule they can join **Jan 1, 2031** (Pmbl., 91 FR 50128). [C3] M
  - **SNF 3-day waiver window:** SNF stay **within 90 days** of anchor discharge or procedure → **within 30 days**, "to maintain a continuation of the existing waiver policy established by the CJR Model" (Reg. §512.695(b)(1)–(2); Pmbl., 91 FR 50240). [C3] M
  - **Participant definition:** "paid under both IPPS and OPPS" → "**eligible to be paid** under both", so actual payment in a given year is not required (Reg. §512.605; Pmbl., 91 FR 50130–50131). [C3] M
  - **Beneficiary eligibility:** tested "upon admission" → tested over **a 180-day lookback ending the day before** the anchor (Reg. §512.620(a)). [C3] M
  - **Safety-net definition:** share of "FFS LEJR **inpatient** episodes" to duals → share of "**Medicare LEJR episodes**" to duals (Reg. §512.605). [C3] M
  - **Outpatient-to-inpatient 3-day rule:** now limited to an admission "at the same CJR-X participant" for the same episode type (Reg. §512.630(c)(2)). [C3] M
  - **Termination process:** a separate CMS termination provision was added, with a 10-day window to request review and a 30-day CMS response (Reg. §512.610(c)). [C3] M
  - **Extreme and uncontrollable circumstances (EUC):** the preamble says cyberattacks were added to the EUC cancellation policy (Pmbl., 91 FR 50146). **Contradiction (L):** the codified text at §512.630(e)(3) lists only §1135 emergency areas combined with a Stafford Act declaration and does not mention cyberattacks (91 FR 50355–50356).
  - **Unchanged:** discount factor (2%), stop-loss and stop-gain (20%, or 5% stop-loss for special hospitals), low-volume threshold (fewer than 31 episodes), the five quality measures, the exclusions (TEAM, Maryland), the collaborator list, and data sharing (Pmbl. "finalizing without modification" statements at 91 FR 50186, 50188, 50204, 50162).
  - **Rejected by CMS:** an upside-only PY1, a glide path, safety-net upside-only years, voluntary participation for TEAM graduates, and a fixed model end date (Pmbl., 91 FR 50127–50128, 50186, 50204).
- **Performance-year calendar [Established] [C3] H.**
  - A PY is "a 12-month period beginning on January 1 and ending on December 31" (Reg. §512.605). PY1 is CY2028.
  - **The model has no end date.** CJR-X is a Phase II expansion under §1115A(c), and CMS refused to set an end date (Pmbl., 91 FR 50128). The RIA counts only five PYs and notes that "the model does not have an end date" (91 FR 50436).
- **Baseline [Established] [C3] M.**
  - A rolling 3-year baseline, weighted 17%, 33% and 50% from oldest to newest year (Reg. §512.640(b)(2)–(3)).
  - PY1 baseline is CY2024–CY2026, and it rolls forward one year each PY.
- **Publication cadence for target prices, data and lists.**
  - **Preliminary target prices:** CMS sends written notice "prior to the start of the performance year" (Reg. §512.640(b)(9)). No specific date is set. [C3] M
  - **Baseline claims data and regional aggregate data:** released annually, "at least one month prior to every performance year" (Reg. §512.665(b)(6)(i), (d)(1)(i)). [C3] M
  - **Exclusions list:** CMS will post it on the CJR-X site "closer to the time when CMS constructs preliminary target prices" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50142–50143). [C3] M
  - **Participant list:** the preamble says CMS "expect[s] to post a list of the CJR-X participants on the CJR-X website for the 2028 performance year/2030 payment year by the end of 2026," with quarterly updates (Pmbl., 91 FR 50131). **It has been posted** ([CJR-X model page][CMS-CJRX]; [participant list file][CJRX-LIST]); see Section 2. [C3] H
  - **Reconciliation:** run "six months after the end of each performance year" using claims available at 6 months (Reg. §512.650(b)(1), (c)(1)). The participant then has 30 days to file a notice of calculation error (Reg. §512.660(b)). [C3] M
  - **Quality results:** PY1 quality is publicly reported in CY2029 (Reg. §512.635(e)(2)). [C3] M
- **Chief Actuary certification [Announced, then incorporated into the rule] [C3] M.**
  - The OACT certification memo on the CJR-X page is **dated August 21, 2026**, after the final rule's August 4 publication ([OACT memo][OACT]). Both the proposed and final rules already say the Chief Actuary "has certified" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50112).
  - **Contradiction (L):** the memo's date postdates the rule. It may be a re-issued or posted version. Treat this as a document-control discrepancy, not a substantive one.
- **The CMS model page lags the file it hosts [C3] M.** As of October 6, 2026 the page still lists "Stage: Announced" and "Participants: N/A", even though it hosts the participant list ([CJR-X page][CMS-CJRX]).

### Inferences
- About 15 months remain before episodes start. The binding near-term dates are a CMS "Primary Point of Contact" form (no posted deadline), the CEHRT attestation before PY1, baseline data by about December 1, 2027 at the latest, and preliminary target prices before January 1, 2028.
- Reconciliation for PY1 will occur around mid-2029, and the cash effect will land in 2029–2030, consistent with the rule's phrase "2028 performance year/2030 payment year".

### Gaps

- The quality-measure performance-period table (Table X.C.-01 is an image at 91 FR 50160 and could not be read from the text).
- Due dates for the Primary Point of Contact form, the CEHRT attestation, the data-request window and the financial arrangements list are "specified by CMS". None is published yet.

## 2. Applicability (A3)

### Takeaway
CJR-X is mandatory for every acute care hospital (a §1886(d)(1)(B) hospital) in the 50 states, DC or a territory that initiates LEJR episodes and is eligible for payment under both IPPS and OPPS. **The only codified exclusions are TEAM participants and Maryland hospitals.** CAHs, REHs, IHS and Tribal hospitals, and Rural Community Hospital Demonstration hospitals fall outside the participant *definition*. **No rural, low-volume or safety-net exemption from participation exists.** Those hospitals get pricing and risk protections instead. A TEAM hospital is excluded only while it is a TEAM participant. It becomes a CJR-X participant automatically when TEAM ends (December 31, 2030, so from January 1, 2031) or as soon as it stops meeting the TEAM participant definition. A participant list exists: 2,217 CCNs as of September 30, 2026, of which **50 are New York CCNs**. A separate TEAM list shows **78 New York CCNs**, and no CCN appears on both lists. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **Participant definition [Established, effective Jan 1, 2028] [C3] H.** A participant is "an acute care hospital located in any of the 50 United States, District of Columbia, or U.S. Territories that initiates LEJR episodes and is eligible to be paid under both the IPPS and OPPS, unless it meets an exception in §512.610(b)" (Reg. §512.605, 91 FR 50351). "Acute care hospital" means a provider paid under §412.1(a)(1). "Hospital" means a §1886(d)(1)(B) hospital, so psychiatric and cancer hospitals are excluded (Pmbl., 91 FR 50129).
- **Mandatory participation [Established] [C3] H.** "CJR-X participants … must participate in CJR-X, except as specified in paragraph (b)" (Reg. §512.610(a)(1), 91 FR 50353). The CJR-X page agrees ([CJR-X page][CMS-CJRX]).
- **Codified exclusions [Established] — verbatim text of §512.610(b) [C3] H:** "(b) *Exclusions.* CMS excludes from CJR-X hospitals that meet any of the following criteria: (1) Is a TEAM participant as defined at §512.505. (2) Is located in the State of Maryland." (91 FR 50353). The CJR-X page lists the same exemptions plus hospitals not paid under both IPPS and OPPS ([CJR-X page][CMS-CJRX]).
- **Exclusions that flow from the definition [Established via preamble interpretation] [C3] H.**
  - The IPPS-plus-OPPS test excludes IHS and Tribal hospitals (paid under IPPS but not OPPS).
  - It also excludes Rural Community Hospital Demonstration hospitals, CAHs and Rural Emergency Hospitals, because they are "not paid under IPPS" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50129–50131).
  - Trade summaries list the same set ([Milliman][MILLIMAN]; [AAHKS summary][AAHKS]). ASCs were considered and are not participants (Pmbl., 91 FR 50130).
- **TEAM treatment [Established] [C3] H.**
  - The exclusion "applies to both mandatory and voluntary TEAM participants … this exclusion would expire at the conclusion of the TEAM test or if at any point a TEAM participant no longer meets the TEAM participant definition, at which point TEAM participants that meet CJR-X participant definition … would become CJR-X participants" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50131).
  - TEAM runs January 1, 2026 to December 31, 2030 (Pmbl., 91 FR 49574). CMS will let TEAM graduates start CJR-X on January 1, 2031 (91 FR 50128), and the RIA assumes TEAM hospitals enter in 2031, which is CJR-X PY4 (91 FR 50437).
  - CMS rejected requests to let TEAM hospitals elect voluntarily after TEAM: "defaulting TEAM hospitals to the participation requirements applicable under CJR-X is appropriate under the models as currently designed" (91 FR 50132).
- **Maryland [Established] [C3] H.** Excluded because of its rate-setting system. CMS notes Maryland's rate-setting authority "will conclude at the end of 2027" and may revisit the exclusion in future rulemaking (Pmbl., 91 FR 50131).
- **AHEAD states are not excluded [Established] [C3] M.** CMS declined a commenter's request to exclude AHEAD-state hospitals, and CJR-X allows concurrent AHEAD participation (Pmbl., 91 FR 50131–50132).
- **Rural, low-volume and safety-net hospitals are not exempt [Established] [C3] H.**
  - Low-volume hospitals (fewer than 31 baseline episodes) remain participants but get no target price and no reconciliation for that PY (Reg. §512.640(a)(4)); see Section 7.
  - Rural hospitals, MDHs, SCHs and safety-net hospitals remain participants with a **5% stop-loss** cap (Reg. §512.650(c)(6)(iii)).
  - Safety-net status also feeds a hospital-level risk adjuster (Reg. §512.645(a)(5)). CMS described this as protection "rather than exempting certain hospitals from accountability" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50115).
- **Participant list exists [Announced / CMS operational file] [C3] H.**
  - The CMS CJR-X page hosts "cjrx-participant-list.xlsx" ([CJR-X page][CMS-CJRX]; [file][CJRX-LIST]). Its header reads: "Acute care hospitals, as of September 30, 2026, identified by CMS Certification Number (CCN), that satisfy the CJR-X participant definition, as defined in 42 CFR 512.605," and says CMS will update it "on a regular cadence."
  - File server last-modified: October 5, 2026. My count: **2,217 CCNs in 49 states, DC and territories combined. There are no Maryland rows and 50 New York rows** (also NJ 17, PA 106, CT 23).
- **TEAM list, used for cross-checking [Announced / CMS operational file] [C3] H.**
  - The TEAM participant list (data as of August 18, 2026) has 721 CCNs, 10 of them voluntary ([TEAM participant list][TEAM-LIST]).
  - **New York CCNs (prefix 33) on the TEAM list number 78:** New York-Newark-Jersey City CBSA 65, Buffalo-Cheektowaga 8, Oneonta 2, Batavia 1, Cortland 1, Massena-Ogdensburg 1.
  - **No CCN appears on both the TEAM and CJR-X lists**, which is consistent with §512.610(b)(1). This is my own cross-check of the two CMS files.
  - TEAM participation end dates on the list read 12/31/2030.
- **Contrary or stale secondary sources [C3] M.** Some trade articles still describe an October 2027 start, or put the end-of-TEAM exclusion in proposed-rule terms. One analytics vendor's article has a January 2028 headline but October 2027 body text ([Medinsight][MEDINSIGHT]). The final rule controls.

### Inferences
- **For the Client's IPPS hospital(s):** whether they are required or exempt is decided entirely by CCN. If a hospital CCN is on the TEAM list (in New York, the NYC metro CBSA, which includes Long Island and the lower Hudson Valley counties in that CBSA, or Buffalo, Oneonta, Batavia, Cortland or Massena-Ogdensburg), it is **exempt from CJR-X through December 31, 2030**. It then **enters CJR-X automatically on January 1, 2031**, unless future rulemaking changes that. If the CCN is on the CJR-X list (the other 50 New York hospitals), it is **required from January 1, 2028**. No other exemption path is available to a New York IPPS/OPPS hospital: New York is not Maryland, and rural, safety-net and low-volume status protect but do not exempt. **This closes the "exempt via some other route" path.**
- A health system with hospitals in different CBSAs could have some hospitals in TEAM (30-day episodes, five episode types) and others in CJR-X (90-day LEJR episodes). Commenters raised this "health systems with hospitals in both" scenario, and CMS declined phase-in options (91 FR 50132).
- In TEAM-heavy markets such as NYC and northern NJ, the CJR-X regional benchmark for the Middle Atlantic census division (NY, NJ, PA) will be built from non-TEAM hospitals only, because §512.640(b)(1) excludes §512.610(b) hospitals from regional spending. Whether that raises or lowers prices for upstate New York cannot be determined from available data.

### Gaps
- **Client:** the CCN(s) of the affiliated IPPS hospital(s) and their CBSA(s), so they can be matched against both CMS files.
- CMS has not said how a hospital is treated if it leaves TEAM mid-year (for example after a merger or CCN change), beyond the general rule that the exclusion expires when TEAM status ends.
- The "Primary Point of Contact Identification Form" deadline is not posted.

## 3. Episode definition

### Takeaway
An episode is a hip, knee or ankle replacement billed as an inpatient case under MS-DRG 469, 470, 521 or 522, or as a hospital outpatient TKA (CPT 27447) or THA (CPT 27130). It is triggered at the participant hospital and runs through day 90 after discharge or the procedure. All Part A and B spending is included, minus defined unrelated or high-cost exclusions. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **Procedures [Established] [C3] H.** "LEJR means any hip, knee, or ankle replacement that is paid under MS-DRG 469, 470, 521, or 522 through the IPPS or HCPCS code 27447 or 27130 through the OPPS" (Reg. §512.605; §512.625(a)). The CJR-X page FAQ lists the same codes ([CJR-X page][CMS-CJRX]).
  - Ankle replacement is captured only through the inpatient MS-DRGs.
  - Outpatient ankle replacement is not an outpatient trigger, because only 27130 and 27447 are listed. This is my inference from the code list.
  - Hip-fracture DRGs 521 and 522 are included.
- **Trigger [Established] [C3] M.**
  - An episode starts with admission for an anchor hospitalization (IPPS claim) or receipt of an anchor procedure (OPPS claim) at a CJR-X participant (Reg. §512.630(c)).
  - If the patient is admitted within 3 days of an outpatient procedure for the same episode type at the same participant, the episode start date is the outpatient date and no separate anchor procedure episode is created (§512.630(c)(2)).
  - Attribution goes to the hospital where the anchor occurs (§512.630(b)).
- **Window [Established] [C3] H.** "An episode ends on the 90th day following the date of the anchor procedure or the date of discharge from the anchor hospitalization … [that date] being counted as the first day" (Reg. §512.630(d)).
- **Beneficiaries [Established] [C3] M.** Inclusion is tested over a 180-day lookback. The beneficiary must have Medicare Parts A and B, Medicare as primary payer, no ESRD-based eligibility, no Medicare Advantage or other managed care, and no UMWA plan (Reg. §512.620(a)).
- **Included services [Established] [C3] M.** All Part A and B items and services, including physician services, readmissions, IPF, LTCH, IRF, SNF, HHA, hospital outpatient care, outpatient therapy, lab, DME, Part B drugs and hospice. Part B professional claims in the 3 days before an anchor hospitalization are also included in the outpatient-converted-to-inpatient case (Reg. §512.625(b)).
- **Excluded services [Established] [C3] M** (Reg. §512.625(c)):
  - Readmissions grouping to oncology, trauma unrelated to the episode, organ transplant or ventricular shunt.
  - MDCs 02, 14, 15 and 25.
  - New technology add-on payments (NTAP), device pass-through payments and hemophilia clotting factors.
  - Low-volume or high-cost Part B drugs: billed in fewer than 31 baseline episodes, or averaging more than $25,000 per episode, plus new-HCPCS rules.
  - The full exclusions list is posted by CMS and updated through rulemaking (§512.625(d)–(e)).
- **Cancellation [Established] [C3] M.** An episode is cancelled if the beneficiary stops meeting the criteria, dies, falls under the EUC policy, or receives an LEJR at a CJR-X hospital during a TEAM 30-day post-discharge window (Reg. §512.630(e)).
- **Post-episode spending [Established] [C3] M.** If a participant's average spending in the 30 days after the episode exceeds the regional mean by more than 3 standard deviations, the excess is subtracted from its NPRA, outside the stop-loss cap (Reg. §512.650(c)(7)). The 30-day window is from Pmbl. X.C.2.f.(5)(h) and the RIA. The regulatory text says "post-episode spending amount" without a day count; see Gaps.

### Inferences
- With the 90-day window, SNF, IRF and home health use plus readmissions dominate the controllable spend. These are the same drivers the CJR evaluation identified (Section 9).

### Gaps
- The length of the post-episode window is not stated in §512.650(c)(7). The CJR heritage and evaluation references point to 30 days (the PY6–8 report cites a "30 days after the episode" spending measure), but the codified text should be confirmed in CMS specifications.
- The actual exclusions list (MS-DRG, MDC and HCPCS) is not yet posted.

## 4. Payment mechanics

### Takeaway
CJR-X is retrospective, two-sided risk from day one. CMS sets **regional** target prices (by census division, MS-DRG/HCPCS type and region, with no hospital-specific blend) from a 3-year weighted baseline. Prices are trended with a regional/national blended trend and adjusted at reconciliation by a TEAM-style beneficiary- and hospital-level risk model, capped normalization (±5%) and a capped retrospective trend (±3%). A **2% discount** is reduced to 1% or 0% for good or excellent quality. **Stop-loss and stop-gain are 20%**, with a 5% stop-loss for rural, MDH, SCH and safety-net hospitals. Reconciliation runs 6 months after each calendar-year PY. There is no glide path and no upside-only year. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **Regional benchmark [Established] [C3] H.**
  - Preliminary target prices are "based on average baseline episode spending for the region," where region is the census division of the CCN's primary address. Regional spending excludes §512.610(b) hospitals (Reg. §512.640(b)(1); §512.605 "Region").
  - Separate prices are set for each MS-DRG, and outpatient 27130 and 27447 are priced with MS-DRG 470 (§512.640(a)(1)).
  - The OACT memo describes benchmarks "broken out by geography and Diagnosis-Related Group" ([OACT][OACT]). New York falls in the Middle Atlantic division ([Census regions][CENSUS]).
- **No hospital-specific blend [Established] [C3] M.** The regulatory text uses only regional baseline spending. The original CJR blended hospital and regional spending before moving to 100% regional in PYs 4–8 (Pmbl., 91 FR 50110).
- **Baseline weighting and outliers [Established] [C3] M.**
  - Baseline years are weighted 17%, 33% and 50% (§512.640(b)(3)).
  - Spending is capped at the 99th regional percentile per episode type and year (§512.640(b)(4)).
  - Spending is standardized for incentive programs and add-ons (§512.640(b)(5)).
- **Trend [Established] [C3] H.**
  - The prospective trend is the average of a regional and a national log-linear multiplier (§512.640(b)(7)).
  - The retrospective trend is capped so it differs from the prospective trend by no more than 3% (§512.645(f)(1)). The OACT memo agrees ("capped at +/- 3 percent").
- **Normalization [Established] [C3] M.** A prospective normalization factor is set at the MS-DRG/HCPCS-region level. The final factor at reconciliation "cannot exceed +/-5 percent of the prospective normalization factor" (§512.640(b)(6); §512.645(e)).
- **Risk adjustment, TEAM-derived [Established] [C3] M** (§512.645(a)).
  - Beneficiary-level factors:
    - HCC count (0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or more; 180-day lookback).
    - Age bracket (<65, 65–74, 75–84, 85+).
    - Economic factor (national Community Deprivation Index (CDI) above the 80th percentile, LIS, or full Medicaid).
    - 24 specific flags covering prior joint procedures, disability as the original reason for entitlement, prior post-acute care use, and HCCs including morbid obesity, dementia, heart failure, COPD, CKD 4/5 and hip fracture.
  - Hospital-level factors: bed size (≤250, 251–500, 501–850, 851+) and safety-net status.
  - Coefficients come from an annual regression on the baseline.
  - CMS says TEAM "incorporates a more comprehensive set of risk adjustment factors" than the CJR Extension, and CJR-X adopts that approach (Pmbl., 91 FR 50112).
- **Discount factor [Established, unchanged from proposal] [C3] H.**
  - 2% applied to the preliminary target price (§512.640(b)(8)).
  - CMS reduced it from the 3% used in CJR and BPCI Advanced because "a 3 percent discount would not be sustainable for an expanded model" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50184–50185).
  - The quality adjustment sets an effective discount of 0% when CQS ≥17.1, 1% when CQS is 12.1–17.0, and 2% when CQS is 6.1–12.0. **At CQS ≤6.0 the discount stays at 2% and the hospital is ineligible for any reconciliation payment** while still owing repayments (§512.645(h); §512.650(d)(1)).
  - OACT assumed a quality distribution of 20% below acceptable, 30% acceptable, 30% good and 20% excellent, giving an average discount of about 1.3% ([OACT][OACT]; RIA, 91 FR 50437).
- **Stop-loss and stop-gain [Established, unchanged] [C3] H.**
  - Repayment and reconciliation payment are each capped at 20% of the aggregated reconciliation target price (§512.650(c)(6)(i)–(ii)).
  - Repayment is capped at **5%** for MDHs, rural hospitals (§412.64 rural or §412.103 reclassified), safety-net hospitals and SCHs (§512.650(c)(6)(iii)). Stop-gain stays at 20% for these hospitals (Pmbl., 91 FR 50204).
  - "Safety net hospital" means the top quartile in its region for share of Medicare LEJR episodes to full-dual beneficiaries during the baseline (§512.605).
  - **Discrepancy (L):** the OACT memo describes the 5% stop-loss only for "rural hospitals and hospitals with a high percentage of duals" and omits MDH and SCH. The regulation controls ([OACT][OACT]).
- **Glide path and safety-net provisions [Established, by rejection] [C3] H.**
  - CMS rejected an upside-only PY1 (91 FR 50127), a discount phase-in or glide path (91 FR 50186), and an upside-only glide path for safety-net hospitals ("We do not believe an upside-only glide path for safety net hospitals is necessary", 91 FR 50204).
  - The safety-net protections are the risk adjuster and the 5% stop-loss, described as "an ongoing feature of the model."
- **Low-volume hospitals:** no target price and no reconciliation (§512.640(a)(4)); see Section 7. [C3] H
- **Reconciliation timing [Established] [C3] H.**
  - Reconciliation runs 6 months after the end of each PY using claims available at that point (§512.650(b)(1), (c)(1)).
  - The reconciliation report covers spending, target prices, CQS, the quality-adjusted amount, stop-loss and stop-gain, NPRA, post-episode spending and the final payment or repayment (§512.650(e)).
  - A notice of calculation error is due within 30 days, followed by reconsideration under §512.190 (§512.660).
  - Preliminary prices are updated at reconciliation for subsequent IPPS and OPPS rule changes (§512.645(g)).
- **Model-level economics [Established, RIA estimate] [C3] M.**
  - The RIA projects net Medicare savings of **about $736 million over 5 PYs**: $129M in PY1, $133M in PY2, $137M in PY3, $166M in PY4 and $171M in PY5.
  - CMS expects to pay $1.463B in reconciliation payments and collect $1.855B in repayments, assuming a 1% behavioral reduction (91 FR 50436–50437).
  - OACT estimates that with no behavior change, hospitals would owe on average about 0.6% of LEJR episode spending ([OACT][OACT]).
  - The $736M figure is also reported by secondary sources ([HIT Consultant][HITC]).

### Inferences
- The aggregate cash flows (about $1.86B in repayments against $1.46B in payments over 5 years) mean CMS expects the median hospital to repay. The pricing alone (a 2% discount, an average 1.3% effective discount, and 20% of hospitals expected to score below acceptable) is designed to produce net repayment unless the hospital changes care patterns. This is an inference from CMS's own projections, not a stated policy.
- For a hospital with no prior bundled-payment experience, quality performance (the CQS bands) directly moves the discount by up to 2 percentage points of target price. That makes HCAHPS and complication performance financially material.

### Gaps
- Actual target-price levels for the Middle Atlantic division (not published until late 2027).
- Whether the Client's hospital qualifies as rural (§412.103), MDH, SCH or safety-net for the 5% stop-loss.

## 5. Quality measures and data reporting (A4)

### Takeaway
There are five measures, all taken from existing CMS quality-reporting submissions and claims. **CJR-X adds no new quality data submission.** The heavy lift is the **THA/TKA PRO-PM**: patient-reported pre-operative and post-operative surveys that are already mandatory under Hospital IQR. Because that measure requires collecting and linking patient data outside claims, it is the one that depends on aggregation. Separately, CJR-X imposes **operational reporting** that is not quality reporting: quarterly financial arrangements and clinician engagement lists, beneficiary-notification logs, public collaborator postings, CEHRT attestation, and gainsharing accounting. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **Measures [Established] [C3] H** (Reg. §512.635(a); CJR-X page lists the same CMIT IDs [CJR-X page][CMS-CJRX]):
  1. Hospital-level RSCR after elective primary THA/TKA (CMIT 350). Inpatient; **claims-based**.
  2. Hospital Visits within 7 Days of HOPD Surgery (CMIT 344, OP-36). Outpatient; **claims-based**.
  3. HCAHPS (CMIT 338). Inpatient; **patient survey**, collected through the hospital's existing HCAHPS vendor and process.
  4. OAS CAHPS (CMIT 162, OP-46). Outpatient; **patient survey** through the existing OQR process.
  5. THA/TKA PRO-PM (CMIT 1618). Applied to inpatient *and* outpatient composites; **patient-reported outcome data submitted by the hospital** under Hospital IQR.
- **Data source and submission [Established, "finalizing without modification"] [C3] H.**
  - Measures 1, 3 and 5 go through existing Hospital IQR processes; measures 2 and 4 through existing Hospital OQR processes. "CJR-X participants would not need to submit additional data for CJR-X" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50161–50162).
  - CMS disagreed that CJR-X needs "significant investments in quality reporting" because hospitals must already report these five measures (91 FR 50162).
- **PRO-PM specifics [Established] [C3] M.**
  - "The THA/TKA PRO-PM became a mandatory requirement for hospitals included in the Hospital IQR Program beginning July 1, 2025" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50157, citing 87 FR 48780).
  - CJR-X does not require outpatient PRO submission. It uses the inpatient PRO-PM to score all episodes.
  - Outpatient PRO reporting to HOQR (OP-42) is voluntary for CY2025 procedures and **mandatory for CY2028 procedures** (2031 payment determination). CJR-X may use it only after further rulemaking (91 FR 50157–50158).
  - CMS acknowledged that "collecting PRO-PM data requires patient engagement, follow-up workflows, and reporting infrastructure" (91 FR 50162).
  - CMS declined to require registry reporting such as AJRR (91 FR 50162).
- **CQS math [Established] [C3] M.**
  - Inpatient composite: complications 50%, HCAHPS 40%, PRO-PM 10%. Outpatient composite: OP-36 50%, OAS CAHPS 40%, PRO-PM 10%. Each composite is capped at 20 points, and so is the overall CQS (§512.635(b); §512.605).
  - Points come from percentile scales: up to 10 for complications and OP-36, 8 for the CAHPS measures, and 2 for PRO-PM. Points are **0 below the 30th percentile** (§512.635(c)).
  - A hospital below the minimum case or survey count (25 cases, or 100 completed surveys for CAHPS) receives **50th-percentile points** for that measure (§512.635(d)).
  - The original CJR also used a 50/40/10 weighting (Pmbl., 91 FR 50163–50168).
- **Drafting anomalies in the codified CQS text, identical in proposed and final (L) [C3]:**
  1. The CAHPS scale awards **5.40 points for the 30th–<40th percentile but 5.00 for the 40th–<50th** (§512.635(c)(2)(vi)–(vii)). This is non-monotonic and probably a typo for 4.40.
  2. The outpatient composite paragraph says the PRO-PM is "weighted at 10 percent of the **inpatient** composite quality score" (§512.635(b)(2)(iii)).
  3. The definitions describe the overall CQS as the "sum of the **weighted average**" of the two composites, while §512.635(b) says it "equals the sum" of them. How inpatient and outpatient are weighted is ambiguous.
  - All three should be confirmed in CMS specifications.
- **Public reporting [Established] [C3] M.** Results appear on a CJR-X-specific CMS web page after a confidential preview. PY1 results are published in CY2029 (§512.635(e)).
- **Non-quality operational reporting [Established] [C3] M:**
  - **Quarterly** financial arrangements list (name, TIN, NPI and dates for every physician, non-physician practitioner or therapist collaborator, collaboration agent and downstream collaboration agent).
  - **Quarterly** clinician engagement list (clinicians with a contractual relationship who take part in CJR-X activities but have no financial arrangement), or an attestation that there are none (§512.615(b)–(d)).
  - Beneficiary notification before discharge, with a producible list of notified patients (§512.622(a)).
  - Collaborator notices (§512.622(b)) and discharge-planning notices of non-covered services (§512.622(c)).
  - A public web page updated at least quarterly with current and historical collaborators and the selection policy (§512.670(d)(1)(ii)).
  - Records of the internal-cost-savings tracking plan, the accounting systems used, and "a description of current health information technology, including systems to track reconciliation payment amounts, repayment amounts, and internal cost savings" (§512.670(d)(2)).
  - Beneficiary-incentive logs for items over $25, and technology retrieval documentation for items over $75 (§512.685(d)).
  - Records retained under §512.135.
- **Inbound CMS data [Established] [C3] M.**
  - Optional, annual request plus a data sharing agreement (DSA) for **raw and/or summary beneficiary-identifiable Part A and B claims**.
  - Baseline data comes annually at least one month before the PY. Performance-year data comes **as frequently as monthly** during the PY and for 6 months after.
  - De-identified regional aggregate data is also provided. Part 2 substance-use data is withheld.
  - The DSA binds downstream business associates to the same terms (§512.665).

### Inferences
- **A4 for an organization running Epic plus many point solutions with no aggregation layer:**
  - The *quality* burden is not new. If the hospital already meets Hospital IQR PRO-PM, HCAHPS and OQR obligations, CJR-X requires nothing more. If its IQR PRO-PM collection is weak, it now also costs money through the CQS and discount.
  - The *managing-to-the-target* burden is new and depends on aggregation. To manage 90-day spending the hospital must ingest monthly CMS raw claims files, attribute them to episodes, join them with Epic encounter data (operating surgeon, discharge disposition, SNF/HHA destination), and track post-acute care spend that happens outside the system.
  - None of the five measures requires the hospital to build new clinical data feeds for CMS. The PRO-PM does require internal pre-op/post-op PROM capture linked to the patient and procedure.
- The quarterly clinician and financial lists, gainsharing accounting (GAAP and Yellow Book standards), and internal-cost-savings documentation imply a durable roster and finance data process across hospital and physician TINs and NPIs. In an IPA context, physician rosters are often held by the IPA rather than the hospital.

### Gaps
- PRO-PM survey windows, instruments and completion-rate thresholds, as specified under Hospital IQR. These were not re-verified in this card.
- **Client:** current Hospital IQR PRO-PM submission status and response rates; whether Epic is configured for PROMIS-10/VR-12 and HOOS Jr./KOOS Jr. capture; and current HCAHPS and complication-measure percentiles.
- CMS's raw-data file layout and delivery channel for CJR-X (not yet published).

## 6. How physicians share in results (A3)

### Takeaway
Only the hospital is the participant and bears risk with CMS. Physicians share in results through a **three-tier set of written arrangements**:
1. The hospital and a **CJR-X collaborator** sign a **sharing arrangement**.
2. A collaborator that is a PGP, NPPGP, TGP or ACO signs **distribution arrangements** with its members or ACO participants.
3. An ACO-participant PGP can make **downstream distributions** to its members.

**Physicians, PGPs and ACOs are named collaborator types.** An "IPA" as such is not a named type. An IPA can take part only if it is itself a Medicare-enrolled PGP, or an ACO meeting the §425.20 definition. Otherwise its member physicians and member PGPs contract directly. **There is no cap on physician gainsharing**, other than that total gainsharing from reconciliation cannot exceed the reconciliation payment. Alignment payments (collaborators sharing downside) are capped at 50% of the hospital's repayment in aggregate, 25% per non-ACO collaborator and 50% per ACO collaborator. **An Advanced APM track exists:** if the hospital attests to CEHRT, physicians on its financial arrangements list or clinician engagement list can be assessed for QP status. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **Collaborator types [Established] [C3] H.** "CJR-X collaborator means an ACO or one of the following Medicare-enrolled individuals or entities that enters into a sharing arrangement": SNF, HHA, LTCH, IRF, **Physician**, Nonphysician practitioner, Therapist in private practice, CORF, Provider of outpatient therapy services, **Physician Group Practice (PGP)**, Hospital, CAH, NPPGP and TGP (Reg. §512.605, 91 FR 50350–50351).
  - "ACO" means an ACO as defined at §425.20 (§512.605). Section 425.20 defines an ACO as a legal entity with its own TIN, formed by ACO participants under §425.102(a) ([eCFR §425.20][ECFR425]).
  - "PGP member" means a physician, non-physician practitioner or therapist who is an owner or employee of the PGP *and has reassigned billing to it* (§512.605).
- **CMS refused to make PGPs participants or conveners [Established] [C3] H.** CMS "did not propose to make PGPs model participants or episode initiators. Nevertheless, physicians, PGPs, and other providers and suppliers may participate through CJR-X collaborator arrangements" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50130).
- **Sharing arrangement requirements [Established] [C3] M** (Reg. §512.670):
  - Written, signed, and entered into **before care is furnished**. Participation must be voluntary.
  - Written **selection policies** that include quality criteria and are not based on volume or value of referrals. A "reasonable minimum number of services" criterion is allowed for quality purposes.
  - Board oversight. A compliance program for both the hospital and the collaborator.
  - The agreement must specify purpose, obligations, dates, eligibility criteria, payment frequency and the formula. It must not induce stinting or restrict device or treatment choice.
- **Gainsharing rules [Established] [C3] M** (Reg. §512.670(c)):
  - Funded **only** from reconciliation payments and/or internal cost savings that are "measurable, actual, and verifiable" under GAAP and Yellow Book standards.
  - Paid **at most once per calendar year**. No loans or advances.
  - The collaborator must meet **episode-related quality criteria set by the hospital**.
  - A non-group collaborator must have **directly furnished** a billable service to a CJR-X beneficiary in that PY. A PGP must have billed for a member's service *and* "contributed to CJR-X activities and been clinically involved," for example through care coordination, care redesign or comorbidity management. ACOs face parallel tests.
  - Amounts must be based **solely on quality and CJR-X activities**. They may reflect a collaborator's relative share of activities.
  - **Aggregate gainsharing from reconciliation money cannot exceed that year's reconciliation payment.**
  - Mandatory recoupment for overpayments or false data. Paid by check, EFT or another traceable method.
- **No physician gainsharing cap [Established] [C3] H.** Commenters "supported CMS' proposal to continue the original CJR Model's policy of no physician gainsharing cap" and noted CJR had eliminated "the 50 percent cap on gainsharing payments in later years"; CMS finalized the framework (Pmbl., 91 FR 50214–50215). The original CJR removed the 50% cap for PYs 6–8 (Pmbl., 91 FR 50111). The codified §512.670 text contains no per-physician cap.
- **Alignment payment caps [Established] [C3] H** (Reg. §512.670(c)(9)–(12)):
  - Alignment payments may be collected only after CMS calculates a repayment.
  - The aggregate from all collaborators may not exceed **50%** of the hospital's repayment.
  - Per collaborator, the limit is **25%** of the repayment for a non-ACO collaborator and **50%** for an ACO collaborator.
  - Confirmed in the preamble at 91 FR 50215.
- **Distribution and downstream distribution [Established] [C3] M** (Reg. §§512.675, 512.680):
  - A collaborator that is an ACO, PGP, NPPGP or TGP may pass gainsharing to its "collaboration agents": PGP members, or ACO participants and providers/suppliers.
  - The arrangement must be written, signed and in place before care. Payments must be based solely on quality and CJR-X activities. The agent must have furnished or billed a service to a CJR-X beneficiary in the PY.
  - Total distributions may not exceed the gainsharing received. A collaborator cannot distribute to someone who has its own sharing arrangement with the same hospital.
  - Downstream: an ACO-participant PGP can pass its distribution on to its members under the same conditions.
- **Anti-kickback protection [Established] [C3] M.** CMS determined that the CMS-sponsored model arrangements safe harbor (42 CFR 1001.952(ii)(1)) is available for compliant gainsharing, alignment and distribution payments. The patient-incentives safe harbor (ii)(2) covers compliant beneficiary incentives (Reg. §512.690).
- **Beneficiary incentives [Established] [C3] M.** In-kind incentives provided by the hospital or its agent that advance a clinical goal: adherence to medication or care plan, fewer readmissions and complications, or chronic disease management. Technology is limited to $1,000 retail value per beneficiary per episode; technology over $75 must be retrieved. Documentation is required above $25. Incentives may not be advertised (Reg. §512.685).
- **Waivers [Established] [C3] M** (Reg. §512.695):
  - **SNF 3-day rule:** waived for SNF admission **within 30 days** of anchor discharge or procedure (proposed: 90 days). The SNF must be on CMS's quarterly list of SNFs rated 3 stars or better for at least 7 of the prior 12 months. Swing beds are exempt from the star test. The hospital is liable if it discharges to a non-qualifying SNF without the required notice.
  - **Telehealth:** geographic and originating-site waivers, home as the originating site, and CJR-X-specific home-visit telehealth codes.
  - **Post-discharge home visits:** up to **9** visits by clinical staff under general supervision for beneficiaries not eligible for home health, billable under Part B by the physician or non-physician practitioner, or by the hospital through reassignment.
- **Advanced APM / CEHRT [Established] [C3] H.**
  - **AAPM option:** the hospital attests annually, before each PY, to using CEHRT as defined at §414.1305, provides its CMS EHR Certification ID before the end of each PY, and keeps documentation. If it does not, CMS assigns it to the non-AAPM option (Reg. §512.615(a)).
  - Under the non-AAPM option, participants may still be APM entities in a MIPS APM (Pmbl., 91 FR 50251–50252).
  - "If a CJR-X participant chose not to attest to CEHRT use, its clinicians would not be assessed for QP status" (91 FR 50251).
  - Clinicians who meet the eligible-clinician definition and are listed on the **financial arrangements list or clinician engagement list** of a hospital using the AAPM option are eligible for QP determinations. CMS's response cites QP thresholds of **75% of Part B payments or 50% of patients** (Partial QP: 50% or 35%) during January 1 – August 31 (91 FR 50252).
  - CMS also explains individual QP determinations through the Affiliated Practitioner List mechanism for clinicians in multiple Advanced APMs, such as MSSP (91 FR 50252).

### Inferences
- **For an IPA:**
  - If the IPA is not itself a Medicare-enrolled group with reassigned billing, which is typical of contracting IPAs, it **cannot be the collaborator**. The workable routes are:
    - (a) member PGPs that are Medicare-enrolled groups each sign sharing arrangements with the hospital and distribute to their physicians;
    - (b) individual physicians sign directly;
    - (c) if the IPA operates or belongs to an MSSP ACO, the **ACO** can be the collaborator and distribute to its ACO participants, with downstream distribution through PGPs, which also carries the higher 50% alignment-payment cap.
  - Whether a non-MSSP ACO, such as a LEAD or REACH entity, meets "ACO as defined at §425.20" is not stated in the regulatory text (L).
- Gainsharing eligibility requires each recipient to have **billed or furnished services to CJR-X beneficiaries in the PY**. Typically that means orthopedists, anesthesiologists and hospitalists. IPA primary care physicians qualify only if they furnish billable care inside the 90-day episode. Distribution amounts cannot track referrals.
- QP qualification through CJR-X alone is unlikely for most physicians. The 75%/50% thresholds measure the whole Part B book, which favors high-volume joint surgeons. CJR-X participation does add Advanced APM exposure for listed clinicians, and an Epic-based hospital can presumably supply a CEHRT ID.

### Gaps
- **Client:** the IPA's legal and Medicare enrollment form (contracting IPA versus enrolled PGP); whether it sponsors or participates in an MSSP, REACH or LEAD ACO; which orthopedic groups operate at the affiliated hospital and whether they are IPA members.
- New York State fee-splitting, corporate-practice and Stark analysis of gainsharing structures was not researched here. The federal safe harbor does not cover state law.
- Whether CMS will publish template sharing or distribution agreements. CMS says none is required and one template may serve multiple models (Pmbl., 91 FR 50208).

## 7. Volume thresholds (A5)

### Takeaway
**There is no minimum to be a participant.** A hospital with **fewer than 31 LEJR episodes in the 3-year baseline** is a "low-volume hospital": it receives no preliminary target price and is not reconciled for that PY. The test is re-run every year as the baseline rolls. There is no performance-year minimum. CMS rejected raising the threshold to 50, 75 or 100. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **Definition and treatment [Established, unchanged from proposal] [C3] H.**
  - "Low-volume hospital means a hospital identified by CMS as having fewer than 31 LEJR episodes during the applicable baseline period" (Reg. §512.605).
  - Such participants "do not receive preliminary target prices for the upcoming performance year and are not eligible for reconciliation for that performance year" (Reg. §512.640(a)(4)).
  - The RIA notes these hospitals "are not held accountable" (91 FR 50436).
- **Rolling test, no performance-year minimum [Established] [C3] M.** CMS considered excluding hospitals with fewer than 10 or 15 episodes in the PY but rejected the idea over steering concerns. It kept a threshold that "update[s] as the baseline shifts forward" and declined to adopt 50, 75 or 100 (Pmbl., 91 FR 50187–50188).
- **Related small-number rules [Established] [C3] M.**
  - Quality measures need 25 cases, or 100 completed surveys for CAHPS; below that the hospital gets 50th-percentile points (§512.635(d)).
  - Drug exclusions use thresholds of 31 baseline episodes and 10 performance-year episodes (§512.625(c)(5)–(6)).
- **TEAM comparison [Established] [C3] M.** TEAM also uses a "31 episodes during the 3-year baseline" low-volume concept, which is cross-referenced in amended TEAM text (91 FR 50349).

### Inferences
- Thirty-one episodes over three years is about 10 a year, a very low bar. Any New York hospital with an active joint program will be fully at risk. The low-volume exemption is relevant only to minimal-volume community hospitals.

### Gaps
- **Client:** CY2024–2026 Medicare FFS LEJR volume by MS-DRG and HCPCS at each affiliated hospital, and the inpatient/outpatient mix.

## 8. Interactions with other models

### Takeaway
TEAM and CJR-X are mutually exclusive at the hospital level. At the beneficiary level, whichever episode starts first takes precedence. **Overlap with MSSP, LEAD and other total-cost-of-care ACOs is allowed with no recoupment:** each entity keeps its own savings, the CJR-X episode spending counts in the ACO's expenditures, and neither model's bonus or penalty flows into the other. BPCI Advanced and CJR have both ended and serve only as design ancestors. CMS will handle future models case by case. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **TEAM [Established] [C3] H.**
  - TEAM hospitals are excluded from CJR-X (§512.610(b)(1)). An LEJR at a TEAM hospital triggers a TEAM episode.
  - An LEJR performed at a CJR-X hospital during a beneficiary's **TEAM 30-day post-discharge** window does not start a CJR-X episode; its spending stays in the TEAM episode (§512.630(e)(4); Pmbl., 91 FR 50211).
  - Conversely, amended TEAM text says a procedure during a **CJR-X episode** at a TEAM hospital does not trigger a TEAM episode; its spending stays in CJR-X (TEAM §512.5xx cancellation criterion: "The beneficiary is in a CJR-X episode and has a procedure …", 91 FR 50347; Pmbl., 91 FR 50207).
  - CMS will add a variable to the monthly data flagging possible TEAM-overlap exclusions (91 FR 50211).
  - CMS does "not envision LEJR episodes being expanded in both concurrently" (91 FR 50131).
- **MSSP and other total-cost-of-care ACOs [Established] [C3] H.**
  - CMS allowed concurrent attribution: "any savings generated on an episode in CJR-X and any contribution to savings in the total cost of care model be retained by each respective participant."
  - CJR-X episode spending is included in the ACO's expenditures, but CJR-X reconciliation amounts are not. ACO shared savings or losses are not included in CJR-X.
  - This applies to MSSP (all tracks, unlike CJR's later carve-out of the ENHANCED track) and to LEAD. CMS imposes no cross-model reconciliation limits beyond CJR-X's own stop-loss and stop-gain (Pmbl., 91 FR 50206–50208).
  - CJR-X has **no primary-care referral requirement** like TEAM's (91 FR 50207).
- **AHEAD and Maryland [Established] [C3] M.** Concurrent AHEAD participation is allowed. Maryland remains excluded (91 FR 50131).
- **BPCI Advanced and CJR legacy [Established] [C3] M.** CJR ran April 1, 2016 to December 31, 2024 (Reg. §512.605 "CJR"). The CJR-X design borrows from BPCI Advanced: low-volume treatment, 99th-percentile caps and trend-cap approaches (Pmbl., 91 FR 50186–50188). No concurrent BPCI Advanced episode overlap is addressed, which is consistent with BPCI Advanced having ended. Its end date was not re-verified in this card.
- **Ambulatory Specialty Model and other future models [Established] [C3] M.** The ASM is mentioned in the preamble's model list (91 FR 50206). CMS "would plan to assess each new model … and would propose a policy in future notice and comment rulemaking" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50207).
- **Incentive programs [Established] [C3] M.** CJR-X payments are independent of existing Medicare incentive and add-on programs (§512.652).

### Inferences
- If the IPA or its physicians participate in an MSSP or LEAD ACO, CJR-X will not claw back ACO savings. A well-run CJR-X episode lowers the ACO's total cost of care for aligned patients who get joint replacements, and the hospital keeps the CJR-X reconciliation. This is the structural basis for an ACO-as-collaborator arrangement, with the 50% alignment cap and distribution rights.

### Gaps
- **Client:** current ACO participation (MSSP track, LEAD, REACH) of the IPA and its physicians, and any TEAM exposure at affiliated hospitals in the NYC or Buffalo CBSAs.

## 9. Track record: CJR evaluation (2016–2024)

### Takeaway
- **PYs 1–5 (2016 to early 2021):** mixed results.
  - About $72M of non-significant savings in PYs 1–4.
  - Then $95.4M of statistically significant losses in PY5, when COVID-era policy waived downside risk.
- **Extension PYs 6–8 (episodes ending October 1, 2021 – December 31, 2024):** net savings.
  - The **seventh annual report (December 2025)** estimated **$112.7M across PYs 6–7**, covering episodes ending October 1, 2021 – December 31, 2023.
  - The **final evaluation report (September 2026)** estimated **$180M across PYs 6–8** (90% CI $30M–$329M), with no adverse change in four claims-based quality measures.
  - The driver was fewer discharges to IRFs, with more patients going home with home health, plus hospital repayments that grew over time.
- **Reconciliation of the secondary-source conflict:** "$112.7M for PY6–7" and "2021–2023" describe the same estimate. PY6 covered episodes ending October 2021 through December 2022, and PY7 covered CY2023. Both are now superseded by the $180M PY6–8 final figure. [C3] H

### Cited Findings
- **PYs 1–5 [Established, as cited by CMS] [C3] M.** "Evaluation results from the first four years … indicated that mandatory hospitals generated $72 million in savings to Medicare while maintaining quality, although the savings were not statistically significant. But in PY 5 … generating $95.4M in statistically significant Medicare losses, due to adjustments made to the model during the COVID-19 PHE" (Pmbl., 91 FR 50111, citing the [CJR Fifth Annual Report][CJR-PY5]).
- **Seventh Annual Report [C3] H.** "CMS Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement Model: Performance Year 7 Evaluation In-Depth Report — Seventh Annual Report", The Lewin Group with GDIT and Telligen, **December 2025** ([PY7 report][CJR-PY7]).
  - "The CJR Model resulted in estimated savings of $112.7 million (M) across PYs 6–7" ($1,142 per episode across 98,744 episodes). The text gives a range of $211.6M to $13.9M; **Exhibit IV-1 says $13.8M** (a minor internal discrepancy, L).
  - PY7 alone: $58.8M ($10.3M–$107.3M).
  - Periods: PY6–PY7 covers episodes ending "October 1, 2021, to December 31, 2023"; PY7 covers episodes ending January 1 – December 31, 2023.
  - The reduction in total episode payments was $975 (90% CI −$2,002 to $52; **p=0.12, not significant at the 10% level**), "mostly driven by reductions in inpatient rehabilitation facility (IRF) payments." Net savings were positive largely because of repayments.
  - "The CJR Model did not lead to changes in quality of care."
  - The final rule relied on this report for the Secretary's expansion finding (Pmbl., 91 FR 50112).
- **Final Evaluation Report (PYs 6–8) [C3] H.** "CMS Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement Model: Performance Years 6–8 Evaluation Report — Final Evaluation Report", The Lewin Group with GDIT and Telligen, **September 2026** ([final report][CJR-FINAL]; [executive summary][CJR-FINAL-ES]; [findings at a glance][CJR-AAG]).
  - "$180 M in net savings for Medicare … (90% CI: $30 M to $329 M)."
  - Composed of a **$136M** episode payment reduction (90% CI −$13M to $286M, **not significant on its own**) plus **$44M** net repayments.
  - Per-episode net savings ranged from $998 in PY6 to $1,530 in PY8, "statistically significant at the 10% level in all 3 years."
  - Repayments per episode rose from $78 (PY6) to $404 (PY7) to $471 (PY8). Episodes: 53,326 (PY6), 45,415 (PY7), 44,628 (PY8).
  - Scale: 34 MSAs, 321 hospitals, more than 143,000 LEJRs, about 9% of LEJRs nationally. 66% of CJR LEJRs were outpatient, compared with 71% nationally.
  - **Drivers:** a 28% decrease in discharges to IRFs, a 32% increase in discharges home with home health, and a $443 reduction in elective per-episode IRF spending (90% CI −$885 to −$1; p=0.10). SNF payments rose for fracture episodes ($1,341; p=0.08).
  - Quality: no adverse changes in complications, mortality, unplanned readmissions or ED use. Both CJR and control hospitals cut readmissions and complications by more than 25% from baseline.
  - The report also found a statistically significant **increase in Medicare spending in the 30 days after the episode** (90% CI $169 to $639).
  - **Document-control flag (L):** the posted executive summary carries a banner reading "Information not releasable to the public unless authorized by law", even though CMS posts it publicly.
- **The CMS CJR-X page states the period as "2021-2023" [C3] M.** It summarizes the CJR evaluation as covering PYs 6 and 7 (2021–2023), and its FAQ savings sentence ("$112.7 million in net savings in while maintaining…") is missing a year ([CJR-X page][CMS-CJRX]). This is consistent with the report's October 2021 – December 2023 window.
- **CMS's own projection assumes some savings were lost after CJR ended [Established, RIA] [C3] M.** The RIA assumes "about half of the savings from the most recent CJR Model evaluation report (3.4 percent) comes back as a cost to Medicare" because former CJR hospitals regressed between CJR's end and CJR-X. It also assumes a 1% behavioral reduction for CJR-X, lower than CJR's, because CJR hospitals had above-average baseline spending and post-acute care use has already declined (91 FR 50437).

### Inferences
- The evidence supports the claim that the CJR *extension design* (regional, trend-capped, quality-adjusted pricing with 20% stop-loss and stop-gain) produced modest net Medicare savings, largely through IRF avoidance and repayments, with stable claims-based quality.
- It is weaker evidence that participating hospitals profited. Repayments rose every year, and CMS projects net repayment from CJR-X participants overall.
- Institutional post-acute care avoidance, especially IRF, is the lever that worked. A market whose IRF and SNF use is already low has less headroom. This is consistent with CMS's own 1% assumption.

### Gaps
- PY1–PY5 report citations were taken from the final rule's characterization; the earlier reports were not re-read here.
- No independent (non-CMS-contractor) evaluation of the CJR extension was reviewed.

## Implications for prepare and position (what the evidence permits — not a recommendation)

- **Required vs. exempt is a CCN lookup with only two outcomes. [C3] H**
  - If the Client's IPPS hospital's CCN appears on the CMS CJR-X list (data as of September 30, 2026), the evidence establishes mandatory two-sided risk from January 1, 2028, with no upside-only year, no glide path and no end date.
  - If the CCN appears on the TEAM list (in New York: NYC metro, Buffalo and four small CBSAs), the hospital is exempt from CJR-X through 2030. The current rule then moves it into CJR-X on January 1, 2031, and CMS rejected voluntary election.
  - No rural, low-volume, safety-net or New York-specific route to *exemption* exists. The evidence closes that path.
  - What remains open: the 5% stop-loss (rural, MDH, SCH or safety-net) and low-volume non-reconciliation (fewer than 31 baseline episodes) apply case by case.
- **Physicians share results only through hospital-led arrangements. [C3] H**
  - The evidence permits positioning the IPA, or its affiliated ACO, as the organizing vehicle for physician alignment, but only in a form the rule recognizes: Medicare-enrolled PGP(s), individual physicians, or an ACO meeting §425.20.
  - An IPA that is purely a contracting entity is not a named collaborator type.
  - No physician gainsharing cap exists, so the economics are bounded only by the hospital's reconciliation payment and internal cost savings. Payments must be quality-based, made at most once a year, and limited to clinicians who furnished episode care.
  - Physicians can take on at most 25% (or 50% through an ACO) of a hospital repayment each, and 50% in aggregate.
- **The Advanced APM pathway exists but is hospital-gated. [C3] H** Physician QP eligibility through CJR-X requires the hospital to attest to CEHRT each year and list the physicians on its quarterly financial arrangements or clinician engagement lists. The evidence permits treating these lists as a joint hospital–IPA deliverable.
- **Data and reporting (A4). [C3] M**
  - The evidence shows CJR-X adds no new *quality* submissions, but the THA/TKA PRO-PM collected under Hospital IQR is now financially weighted.
  - Managing to the target depends on aggregation. It requires ingesting monthly CMS raw claims, linking them to Epic episodes and surgeons, and tracking post-acute care across outside providers.
  - The roster, finance and compliance artifacts are new and recurring: quarterly clinician lists, a public collaborator page, GAAP/Yellow Book gainsharing ledgers, and an internal-cost-savings methodology.
  - For an organization with Epic plus point solutions and no aggregation layer, these are the specific capability gaps the rule creates.
- **Economics. [C3] M** CMS's own RIA projects aggregate participant repayments of about $1.86B against payments of about $1.46B over five years, and OACT expects about 20% of hospitals to score below acceptable on quality. The evidence permits treating CJR-X as a likely net-cost model for an unprepared hospital and a possible net gain only for hospitals that move post-acute care patterns and quality scores. Historically the IRF-to-home shift was the proven lever.
- **Timing. [C3] M** The evidence permits planning against these fixed dates:
  - Point-of-contact form and data-sharing agreement: now through 2027.
  - CEHRT attestation: before January 1, 2028.
  - Baseline data and preliminary target prices: about November–December 2027.
  - Sharing arrangements: signed *before* care is furnished under them, so before January 1, 2028 for PY1 coverage.
  - First reconciliation: about mid-2029.
