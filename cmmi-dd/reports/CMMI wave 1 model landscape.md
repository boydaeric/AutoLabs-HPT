# Mandatory joint bundles arrive as oncology entry stays shut

*Wave 1 report, Research Methodology Reference program. Evidence current to October 8, 2026. Sources: cards C1 (model landscape and eligibility triage), C2 (Enhancing Oncology Model), C3 (CJR-X), and C4 (fit-criteria rubric, no research claims). This is a wave report, not the program synthesis. It makes no recommendation.*

Wave 1 establishes that the Client's affiliated IPPS hospital cannot avoid mandatory lower-extremity joint replacement (LEJR) episode accountability. The only open question is timing. If the hospital's CCN is one of the **78 New York CCNs on the TEAM list**, it is in TEAM now (Jan 1, 2026 to Dec 31, 2030) and moves into CJR-X automatically on **Jan 1, 2031**. If it is one of the **50 New York CCNs on the CJR-X list**, it carries two-sided CJR-X risk from **Jan 1, 2028**, with no upside-only year, no glide path and no end date. No rural, low-volume, safety-net or New York-specific exemption exists [C1, H] [C3] H. The voluntary oncology model, EOM, is effectively closed to new entrants. The last practice application window shut on **Sep 16, 2024**, no third cohort has been announced, the model ends **Jun 30, 2030**, and none of its 23 current participant practices is in New York. Only narrow join-through routes remain: reassigning billing to an existing participant, acting as a Care Partner, or entering a mandatory pool [C1, H] [C2][H]. Across the wider landscape, **no CMS Innovation Center model names an IPA or CIN as a participant type**. An IPA reaches models by sponsoring an ACO legal entity or through its member TINs [C1, H]. The voluntary decisions with live or upcoming windows are LEAD (future windows anticipated; Letter of Interest available now), Geo AHEAD (RFA expected Q1 2027; five downstate counties only), MSSP (a Jan 1, 2028 cycle, inferred), ACCESS (rolling) and MAHA ELEVATE cohort 2 (2027, not yet dated). Overlap rules tie these choices together [C1, H/M]. Most remaining uncertainty turns on facts about the Client rather than gaps in the evidence: the hospital's CCN and CBSA, the IPA's Medicare enrollment form, the oncology TIN structure, current ACO participation, and data infrastructure.

## How to read the tags and status labels

Every substantive claim carries the tag of the card it came from, in that card's own format: `[C1, H]`, `[C2][H]` or `[C3] H`. Grades run H (multiple consistent primary sources), M (one strong source) and L (thin, secondary or contested). The three cards define status labels slightly differently, and this report keeps each card's usage.

| Card | Established | Proposed | Announced |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Operating, or finalized in regulation | In a proposed rule | Stated by CMS, but not yet in regulation or an RFA |
| C2 | In a governing CMS document now in effect, with the effective date stated | CMS says it "may" do this, or flags it as an example | Published by CMS as a future or planned term |
| C3 | Codified in the final rule (CMS-1849-F), with its effective date | Appeared in CMS-1849-P only | CMS web or fact-sheet material that is not in regulation |

Where CMS's own website label differs from a card's definition, both are shown. CJR-X and ASM, for example, are labeled "Announced" on CMS's site but are finalized in regulation.

C4 is a fit-criteria rubric. It was written before Wave 1 evidence came back, so its criteria were not shaped by the findings [C4]. It defines four gates and six profiles. The gates are G1 eligibility path, G2 timing and window, G3 downside exposure, and G4 data and reporting feasibility. The profiles are U financial upside, S physician-share mechanism, O operational burden, I interactions, P policy durability and V option value. A confidence meta-criterion, C, takes the lowest gate grade. C4 also sets decision rules that route voluntary models to decline, defer (with a named trigger), join through a participating organization, or join directly, and route mandatory models to prepare-and-position tiers A–D [C4]. **This report does not score or fill the C4 matrix.** It records the evidence that later threads will score against those criteria. Ledger C keys open Client questions to C4's Q1–Q9 where they match.

## Five mandatory models and five open doors define the landscape

C1 started from CMS's own model dataset rather than an assumed candidate list. That dataset is the "Innovation Center Model Summary Information" feed behind the JavaScript-rendered models page, and its 105 rows break down as 20 Active, 8 Announced, 4 Authorized for Expansion, 9 Performance Period Ended, 61 Not Active and 3 Withdrawn ([CMS model dataset](https://data.cms.gov/data-api/v1/dataset/0d753f51-c3de-43cd-95d2-550a23b8606a/data)) [C1, H]. From it, C1 identified **fourteen models relevant to a New York IPA with an affiliated hospital, plus MSSP as a non-CMMI baseline** [C1, H].

### Mandatory exposure is set by geography and facility

**Five models are mandatory, so the Client's task is preparation, not choice.**

- **TEAM** is established. It was finalized in the FY2025 IPPS final rule and updated in the FY2026 and FY2027 rules. It has run since Jan 1, 2026 in six New York CBSAs: 35620 New York-Newark-Jersey City, 15380 Buffalo-Cheektowaga, 12860 Batavia, 36580 Oneonta, 18660 Cortland and 32390 Massena-Ogdensburg ([CMS TEAM participant list](https://www.cms.gov/team-model-participant-list)) [C1, H].
- **CJR-X** is established. It was finalized in the FY2027 IPPS/LTCH final rule (91 FR 49570, published Aug 4, 2026) and applies nationwide from Jan 1, 2028 to IPPS hospitals not in TEAM ([91 FR 49570](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm)) [C1, H] [C3] H.
- **ASM** is established. It was finalized in the CY2026 PFS final rule. Performance runs Jan 1, 2027 to Dec 31, 2031, and the model applies to individual cardiologists and low-back-pain specialists (identified by TIN and NPI) in **11 upstate CBSAs**. No NYC or Long Island metro division is included ([ASM mandatory geographic areas](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/asm-mandatory-geo-areas.xlsx)) [C1, H].
- **IOTA** is established. It covers adult kidney transplant hospitals in three New York donation service areas (DSAs), with 13 New York transplant hospitals listed ([IOTA participant/DSA list](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/iota-participant-dsa-list.xlsx)) [C1, H].
- **WISeR** excludes New York entirely ([CMS WISeR page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/wiser)) [C1, H].

The geography produces sharply different exposure by location. A Client in Manhattan, the outer boroughs, Westchester or Long Island has TEAM exposure through CBSA 35620 but no ASM exposure. A Buffalo Client has both. An Albany, Rochester or Syracuse Client has ASM exposure, no TEAM exposure, and a hospital that enters CJR-X in 2028 [C1, inference].

### The voluntary paths that are actually open or opening

The open or opening voluntary paths are these:

- **ACCESS:** applications are reviewed on a rolling basis, and new tracks start Apr 1, 2027 ([CMS ACCESS page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/access)) [C1, M].
- **LEAD:** the PY2027 window closed May 17, 2026. CMS "anticipates offering additional application windows for subsequent Performance Years," and a non-binding Letter of Interest is available ([LEAD RFA](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/lead-rfa.pdf)) [C1, H].
- **MSSP:** Phase 1 for a Jan 1, 2027 start closed Jun 23, 2026, with final disposition on Oct 15, 2026. A mid-2027 window for a 2028 start is inferred, not announced ([MSSP timeline](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/fee-for-service-providers/shared-savings-program-ssp-acos/application-types-timeline)) [C1, M].
- **AHEAD and Geo AHEAD:** New York is in Cohort 3 as a downstate sub-state region. The Geo Entity RFA is expected in **Q1 2027**, a technical webinar is set for **Oct 20, 2026**, and performance begins Jan 1, 2028 ([Geo AHEAD preview](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/ahead-geo-spec-prv-entity-bidding.pdf); [CMS AHEAD page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/ahead)) [C1, H].
- **MAHA ELEVATE:** this is an announced cooperative-agreement program, not a payment model. Cohort 1 closed May 15, 2026; cohort 2 starts in 2027 with no NOFO date posted ([CMS MAHA ELEVATE page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/maha-elevate)) [C1, H].

Several models have no window open or expected:

- **ACO REACH** is closed and ends Dec 31, 2026 [C1, H].
- **EOM** has no intake open or announced [C1, H].
- **KCC** has no further solicitations planned [C1, H].
- **ACO PC Flex** is not offering another round [C1, H].
- **GUIDE** has no window found [C1, M].

Four models ended early by Dec 31, 2025: Making Care Primary (New York had been an MCP state), Primary Care First, ESRD Treatment Choices and Maryland TCOC ([CMS Mar 12, 2025 fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-innovation-center-announces-model-portfolio-changes-better-protect-taxpayers-help-americans-live)) [C1, H]. C1 found no new CMMI provider model announced between June and October 2026 [C1, M].

### No model accepts an IPA as itself

The participant definitions route an IPA through other vehicles in every case [C1, H]:

- **Hospitals** are direct participants only in TEAM, CJR-X, IOTA and AHEAD Hospital Global Budgets.
- **ACO legal entities** are the participants in LEAD, MSSP and Geo AHEAD.
- **PGP TINs or Part B-enrolled organizations** are the participants in ACCESS, EOM and GUIDE.
- **ASM** attaches to individual TIN-NPIs.

LEAD's RFA allows Participant TINs that are "Networks of individual practices of physicians" under an ACO "legal entity identified by a federal [TIN]" ([LEAD RFA](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/lead-rfa.pdf)) [C1, H]. Geo Entities "can be provider-led organizations, technology/digital health companies, health plans, hospitals, or other entities" ([Geo AHEAD preview](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/ahead-geo-spec-prv-entity-bidding.pdf)) [C1, H].

C1 infers that a non-billing contracting IPA, which it describes as typical in New York, cannot itself be a Part B-enrolled participant in ACCESS, EOM or GUIDE. It can, however, sponsor or serve as the legal entity for an LEAD, MSSP or Geo AHEAD ACO [C1, inference]. Whether a New York Article 44 or IPA-licensed entity can serve as an ACO legal entity is a legal question no card addressed [C1, gap].

### Exclusivity rules make the voluntary choices one decision

The voluntary total-cost-of-care options cannot be stacked:

- **LEAD and MSSP** are mutually exclusive at the TIN level [C1, H].
- **Geo AHEAD** allows no beneficiary overlap with MSSP, PC Flex, ACO REACH or LEAD, and a Geo Participant TIN cannot be in another ACO model ([Geo AHEAD preview](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/ahead-geo-spec-prv-entity-bidding.pdf)) [C1, H].
- **The episode models sit alongside the ACOs.** CJR-X allows concurrent MSSP (all tracks) and LEAD attribution with no recoupment; each entity keeps its own savings ([91 FR 49570](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm)) [C3] H.
- **EOM** does not claw back its performance-based payment (PBP) against MSSP savings, but generally does against REACH-style ACOs ([2024 EOM RFA](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)) [C2][H].
- **EOM–LEAD** treatment is unpublished. The LEAD RFA says overlap is "likely" permitted [C2][M].

C1 infers that the LEAD PY2028 window and the Geo AHEAD RFA could both fall in roughly the same 2027 timeframe. The LEAD date is unannounced, so this sequencing is an inference, not an established fact [C1, inference].

## EOM is closed to new practices through its 2030 end

### Only a single-TIN oncology group can participate

EOM's participant definition is narrow and explicit. An applicant "must be a Medicare-enrolled oncology PGP identifiable by a unique federal taxpayer identification number (TIN)." It must have at least one Hematology/Oncology or Medical Oncology practitioner ([2024 EOM RFA](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024)) [C2][H]. The FAQ closes the door on organizational intermediaries: "Entities other than PGPs are also ineligible to participate in EOM" ([EOM FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)) [C2][H]. **The IPA, its ACO or a CIN cannot be the participant.** A health-system medical group can apply, but only as a single-TIN PGP. Current participants include health-system practice groups such as Prisma Health University Medical Group and the UNC Faculty Practice ([EOM participant list](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-participants)) [C2][H].

Two features specific to New York matter:

- **No participant is near.** No current participant is based in New York; the nearest is in Connecticut [C2][H].
- **The cancer-hospital referral bar may bite.** PGPs that "routinely refer beneficiaries to PPS-Exempt Cancer Hospitals (PCHs) for chemotherapy services are not eligible" [C2][H]. Two PCHs are believed to be in New York, but that identification comes from background knowledge and is unverified [C2][L].

- the FAQ (modified Sep 22, 2026);

C2 then tested join-through routes and found three narrow ones:

- **Reassigning billing.** Client oncologists could reassign billing to an existing participant's TIN and be added to its Practitioner List, which is updated semiannually [C2][M]. Participation Agreement rules on mid-model additions and acquisitions are not public.
- **Care Partner.** A Client entity could serve as a Care Partner, which carries no PBP or recoupment (PBR) accountability [C2][M].
- **Mandatory pool.** A practice with more than 20% billing overlap with an existing participant can apply to join that participant's mandatory pool before a performance period ([Payment Methodology v7.0, App. J](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)) [C2][H].

ACO membership does not confer EOM participation [C2][H].

A secondary source (AJMC, Apr 2025) reports that a CMS-authored *JCO Oncology Practice* article "signaled possible future mandatory EOM participation." That article was not retrieved [C2][L].

### Downside starts on day one under two risk arrangements

EOM uses six-month total-cost-of-care episodes, triggered by systemic therapy for seven cancer types [C2][H]. The Monthly Enhanced Oncology Services (MEOS) payment is **$110 per beneficiary per month ($140 for dual eligibles)** in both cohorts. Cohort 1 started at $70/$100 and moved to $110 from Jan 1, 2025 ([EOM FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/eom-frequently-asked-questions)) [C2][H].

| | Discount | Stop-loss | Stop-gain | Recoupment threshold | QPP status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RA1 (default) | 4% | 2% of benchmark | 4% | 98% (PP1–PP3); 100% (PP4–PP13) | MIPS APM |
| RA2 | 3% | 6% | 12% | Same | Advanced APM and MIPS APM |

Source: [2024 EOM RFA](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-rfa-2024) [C2][H].

There is **no upside-only option**. Participants may switch arrangements semiannually, and quality can reduce a recoupment by up to 10% [C2][H]. Payment Methodology v7.0 rebases the benchmark for episodes from Jan 1, 2027 onward to a Jan 2019 – Dec 2022 baseline that "excludes most hormonal therapies" [C2][M]. EOM has **no minimum episode volume and no low-volume rule**. Volume matters only through quality-measure minimum denominators, the 50-episode experience-adjuster blend and the 90% clinical-data reporting floor ([Payment Methodology v7.0](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-payment-methodology)) [C2][H].

### The data burden falls where the Client has no aggregation layer

EOM requires eight participant redesign activities: 24/7 access, navigation, care plans, guideline-concordant treatment, HRSN screening, ePROs, data-driven continuous quality improvement, and CEHRT [C2][H]. It also requires three reporting streams [C2][H]:

- **Clinical data elements:** semiannual, for at least 90% of attributed episodes. They include TNM staging and ER/PR/HER2 status, and they feed payment adjusters.
- **Sociodemographic elements**, submitted with the clinical data.
- **Two aggregate quality measures, EOM-4 and EOM-5:** annual MIPS CQM measures, reported "for all qualifying patients under the PGP TIN," not only EOM beneficiaries ([CDE Guide v2.5](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/eom-clinical-data-elements-guide)).

Reporting can be done manually through a pre-populated Excel template, so FHIR integration is not required [C2][H].

The harder dependencies sit elsewhere [C2][H/M]:

- ePRO collection before each qualifying visit. It is required for cohort 2 from **Jul 1, 2027**.
- Real-time record access for after-hours clinicians.
- TIN-wide CQM aggregation.
- Ingesting CMS's monthly claims files to manage total cost of care, including Part D drug spend.

### Results show shrinking enrollment and mixed economics

The participant count has fallen. Cohort 1 started with 44 practices, and cohort 2 added 7. The CMS page now lists **23 PGPs and 1 payer** ([CMS EOM page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/enhancing-oncology-model)) [C2][H]. CMS's cost file shows about 13 terminations through May 2025 ([EOM Cost and Quality file](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/eom-cost-qual-pp1-2.xlsx)) [C2][H].

The share of participants owing money grew across the first three performance periods [C2][H]:

| Period | Earned a PBP | Owed a PBR | Total PBP | Total PBR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PP1 | 29 | 11 | ≈$31.4M | ≈$4.2M |
| PP3 | 21 | 14 | ≈$14.9M | ≈$12.4M |

The first evaluation, covering PP1, estimated a $13.1M payment reduction driven by Part B drugs. After MEOS and incentive payments, that became a **net loss to Medicare of $13.2M (90% CI −$35.1M to +$8.5M)**, with no effect on quality or utilization ([Evaluation Executive Summary](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/data-and-reports/2025/eom-1st-eval-report-exec-sum)) [C2][H]. That evaluation is still the latest one listed [C2][H].

## CJR-X makes the hospital's CCN the only variable

### Final regulation with a fixed start and no end

CJR-X is **established**: CMS-1849-F, FR Doc. 2026-15833, **91 FR 49570–50461**, published Aug 4, 2026. Its rules section took effect Oct 1, 2026, and episodes begin **Jan 1, 2028** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/04/2026-15833/medicare-program-hospital-inpatient-prospective-payment-systems-for-acute-care-hospitals-ipps-and); [GPO text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm)) [C3] H [C1, H].

The final rule changed several terms from the proposal [C3] H/M:

- **Start date:** from Oct 1, 2027 to **Jan 1, 2028**, with calendar-year performance years.
- **PY1 baseline:** now **CY2024–CY2026**.
- **TEAM handoff:** TEAM hospitals now enter CJR-X on Jan 1, 2031 rather than Oct 1, 2031.

CMS **rejected** five requests [C3] H:

- an upside-only PY1;
- a glide path;
- upside-only safety-net years;
- voluntary election for TEAM graduates;
- a fixed end date.

CJR-X is a Phase II expansion with **no end date** (91 FR 50128).

The CMS model page still showed "Stage: Announced" and "Participants: N/A" as of Oct 6, 2026, even though it hosts the participant list ([CJR-X page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/cjr-x)) [C3] M.

### The TEAM exclusion is the only door, and it closes in 2031

The codified exclusions, verbatim from §512.610(b), are: a hospital that "(1) Is a TEAM participant as defined at §512.505. (2) Is located in the State of Maryland" [C3] H. Critical access hospitals (CAHs), rural emergency hospitals (REHs), IHS and Tribal hospitals, and Rural Community Hospital Demonstration hospitals fall outside the participant definition rather than being formally exempted [C3] H. The TEAM exclusion "would expire at the conclusion of the TEAM test or if at any point a TEAM participant no longer meets the TEAM participant definition" (91 FR 50131) [C3] H.

**Rural, low-volume and safety-net hospitals are not exempt.** They receive protections instead [C3] H:

- a **5% stop-loss** for rural hospitals, MDHs, SCHs and safety-net hospitals;
- a safety-net risk adjuster;
- no target price and no reconciliation for low-volume hospitals.

AHEAD-state hospitals are not excluded either [C3] M.

CMS has posted both participant lists, and the two cards agree on them [C3] H [C1, H]:

- **CJR-X list:** **2,217 CCNs** as of Sep 30, 2026, with **50 New York CCNs** and no Maryland rows ([participant list](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/cjrx-participant-list.xlsx)).
- **TEAM list:** dated Aug 18, 2026, with **78 New York CCNs**: 65 in New York-Newark-Jersey City, 8 in Buffalo-Cheektowaga, 2 in Oneonta and 1 each in Batavia, Cortland and Massena-Ogdensburg.
- **No overlap:** C3's cross-check found **no CCN on both lists** [C3] H.

C3 concludes that required-versus-exempt is "a CCN lookup with only two outcomes" and that "this closes the 'exempt via some other route' path" [C3] H.

### Two-sided pricing designed to produce net repayment

CMS sets **regional target prices** by census division (New York falls in the Middle Atlantic division) from a 17/33/50-weighted three-year baseline. There is no hospital-specific blend [C3] H/M. The discount and quality terms work as follows [C3] H:

- **Discount:** a 2% discount drops to 1% or 0% with a good or excellent composite quality score (CQS).
- **Quality floor:** at CQS ≤6.0 the hospital is **ineligible for any reconciliation payment** but still owes repayments.
- **Stop-loss and stop-gain:** both are **20%**, or 5% stop-loss for protected hospitals.
- **Trend and normalization:** the retrospective trend is capped at ±3% and normalization at ±5%.

Reconciliation runs six months after each performance year [C3] H.

CMS's own projections expect participants overall to repay ([91 FR 49570](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm); [OACT certification](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/certification-comprehensive-care-joint-replacement-expansion.pdf)) [C3] M:

- **Net savings:** about **$736M** in net Medicare savings over five performance years.
- **Cash flows:** **$1.463B in reconciliation payments against $1.855B in repayments**.
- **No-behavior-change case:** the Office of the Actuary (OACT) estimates hospitals would owe about 0.6% of LEJR episode spending.

C3 infers that CJR-X is "a likely net-cost model for an unprepared hospital" [C3, inference].

### Physicians share only through hospital-led written arrangements

Only the hospital bears risk with CMS. Physicians share through a three-tier structure [C3] H:

1. sharing arrangements between the hospital and a **CJR-X collaborator**;
2. distribution arrangements from collaborator PGPs or ACOs to their members;
3. downstream distribution from ACO-participant PGPs to their members.

The collaborator list includes **Physician, PGP and "ACO" as defined at §425.20**, along with post-acute providers and others. An **"IPA" is not a named type** ([91 FR 49570](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm); [42 CFR 425.20](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-425/subpart-A/section-425.20)) [C3] H. CMS "did not propose to make PGPs model participants or episode initiators" [C3] H.

The financial limits on sharing are these [C3] H:

- **Gainsharing:** no per-physician cap applies. In aggregate, gainsharing from reconciliation money cannot exceed the hospital's reconciliation payment.
- **Alignment payments** (collaborators sharing the hospital's repayment) are capped at 50% of the repayment in aggregate, 25% for each non-ACO collaborator and **50% for an ACO collaborator**.

The conditions on payments are these [C3] M:

- They are paid at most once a year.
- They are funded only from reconciliation payments or GAAP/Yellow-Book-verifiable internal savings.
- They are based solely on quality and CJR-X activities.
- They go only to clinicians who furnished or billed episode care.

The CMS-sponsored model arrangements safe harbor covers compliant payments [C3] M. **New York fee-splitting and corporate-practice law was not researched** [C3, gap]. Physicians become QP-eligible only if the hospital attests to CEHRT and lists them on its quarterly financial-arrangements or clinician-engagement lists [C3] H.

C3 infers three workable routes for a contracting IPA:

- member PGPs each contract with the hospital;
- individual physicians sign directly;
- an MSSP ACO the IPA operates or belongs to serves as the collaborator.

Whether a LEAD or REACH ACO qualifies as a §425.20 ACO is not stated [C3, L].

### Quality reporting is not new; managing to the target is

All five CJR-X measures come from existing Hospital IQR/OQR submissions or claims. CMS states that participants "would not need to submit additional data for CJR-X" [C3] H. The THA/TKA patient-reported outcome measure (PRO-PM) has been mandatory under IQR since Jul 1, 2025. It now carries financial weight through the CQS, and CMS acknowledges it "requires patient engagement, follow-up workflows, and reporting infrastructure" [C3] M.

The new burden is operational and depends on aggregation [C3] M/inference:

- quarterly financial-arrangements and clinician-engagement lists;
- beneficiary-notification logs;
- a public collaborator web page updated quarterly;
- gainsharing accounting;
- monthly CMS raw-claims files under a data sharing agreement (DSA), which must be joined with Epic encounter data and outside post-acute spend.

**The low-volume rule is a very low bar.** A hospital with **fewer than 31 LEJR episodes in the three-year baseline** gets no target price and no reconciliation. The test re-runs annually, and CMS rejected raising it to 50, 75 or 100 [C3] H.

### The CJR track record: modest savings, driven by repayments and less IRF use

The CJR evaluations, carried out for CMS by the Lewin Group, show savings only in the later years [C3] M/H:

| Period | Medicare result | Significance and drivers |
|---|---|---|
| PYs 1–4 | ~$72M savings | Not statistically significant |
| PY5 | $95.4M losses | Statistically significant; downside risk had been waived during the COVID-19 emergency |
| PYs 6–8 (final report, Sep 2026) | **$180M net savings** (90% CI $30M–$329M) | See below |

The PYs 6–8 result breaks down as follows ([CJR final evaluation](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/data-and-reports/2026/cjr-py6-8-annual-rpt)) [C3] H:

- **Components:** a $136M episode payment reduction, not significant on its own, plus $44M in net repayments.
- **Repayments per episode:** rose from $78 to $404 to $471 across the three years.
- **Drivers:** IRF discharges fell 28%, and discharges home with home health rose 32%.
- **Quality:** no adverse change in quality.
- **Post-episode spending:** Medicare spending in the 30 days after the episode rose significantly.

C3 draws two inferences. The evidence supports modest Medicare savings from the extension design. It is weaker evidence that participating hospitals profited [C3, inference].

## What the evidence permits relative to the decision anchor

This section states what Wave 1 evidence permits for the decision anchor. It recommends nothing.

**For EOM (voluntary):**

- **Direct participation by the IPA, an ACO or a CIN is prohibited** [C2][H].
- **Direct entry by a Client oncology PGP is closed.** No window has existed since Sep 16, 2024, and none is announced [C2][H].
- **Joining through an existing participant is possible only in narrow forms.** Each depends on a specific participant's willingness and on unpublished agreement terms, and no participant is in New York [C2][H/M].

The evidence leaves deferral or declining as the options it supports. The deferral trigger the evidence itself names is "CMS announces a third cohort or a successor oncology model." CMS has indicated neither, and the possibility of a mandatory successor rests on a single unretrieved article [C2][L].

**For CJR-X (mandatory):**

- **Required-versus-exempt is a CCN lookup** with two outcomes: required from Jan 1, 2028, or deferred to Jan 1, 2031 through TEAM. No exemption path exists [C3] H.
- **The rule defines the physician-side position.** Sharing runs through enrolled PGPs, individual physicians or a §425.20 ACO, with no per-physician gainsharing cap and a 50% alignment cap for an ACO collaborator [C3] H.
- **Preparation runway is fixed.** It is about 15 months to PY1. Sharing arrangements must be signed before care is furnished, and target prices arrive around Nov–Dec 2027 [C3] M.

**For the rest of the landscape:**

- TEAM, ASM and IOTA status are each resolved by a single Client fact.
- LEAD, MSSP and Geo AHEAD form one mutually exclusive total-cost-of-care choice, with entry points concentrated in 2027.
- ACCESS is open on a rolling basis through enrolled TINs.

All of these are subject to the Ledger C items above [C1, H/M].

## Conclusion

Wave 1 changes the shape of the decision more than its content. Of the two models the program set out to test, one has nothing left to decide. CJR-X's applicability, terms and timeline are fixed in codified regulation, so the open questions are execution questions: physician-share structure, PRO-PM performance and claims-aggregation capability. The other model, EOM, has almost no door left to decide about. The live strategic choice has moved to models the program had not centered: a single TCOC vehicle decision among LEAD, MSSP and (if downstate) Geo AHEAD, whose overlap rules and 2027 windows interact. Running through every model is one structural fact: the IPA as a contracting entity is never the participant. That makes its legal and Medicare enrollment form the single most consequential undisclosed Client fact.

The evidence also bounds what any later thread can claim. The two strongest upside datasets come from CMS contractors, and both show Medicare savings coexisting with rising participant repayments (CJR) or rising recoupments and attrition (EOM). CMS's own CJR-X projections assume participants collectively repay more than they receive. Any later positioning argument for the Client will therefore rest on post-acute redesign and quality-score execution, not on model generosity. For three items the gap is in the evidence itself, not in the Client facts: ASM exemptions, the TEAM collaborator scope, and whether a LEAD ACO counts as a CJR-X collaborator. All three should be closed in primary text before Wave 2 scores the C4 matrix.
