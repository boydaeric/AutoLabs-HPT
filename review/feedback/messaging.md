## How this review reads the article and the evidence

**The central argument, as v16 will stand once fixed.**

* **Headline claim.** Under CMS's central estimates, the two proposed rules leave providers about $488 billion to $753 billion worse off over 2026–2035 (2026 dollars), net of lower provider taxes. No published figure measures that combined net loss.
* **Reader's takeaway.** The figures in circulation for the two rules ($56.6 billion to $681 billion) each answer a narrower question or use a different basis. The two rules' estimates overlap by $241.9 billion, so they cannot simply be added.
* **Call to action.** A hospital can estimate its own position from its SDP receipts and its provider tax and IGT payments (sec. 7), and should put the sec. 7 questions to its state Medicaid agency.

**Verification rule.** A finding, or any part of one, whose status is unverifiable, not found or not checked is treated as hold-level evidence. When the analyst records a passage as *located*, that verifies what the commenter wrote. It does not verify the commenter's claims about anyone else. Where a use would rest on such a claim and nobody checked it, that part is treated as not checked. F-015 is used as corrected.

**Quote budget.** v16 already quotes CMS-2449-P (91 FR 30453, sec. 4). It also quotes CMS-2452-P twice: the title of Table 12 and CMS's cross-reference to Table 12 at 91 FR 46592 (sec. 5). Under section 5 of the conventions, any finding resting on either Federal Register document (F-005, F-007, F-011, F-012, F-015) goes in as a paraphrase with a pin cite, unless the writer frees that document's quote slot. F-011 is the strongest candidate for the slot.

### F-007: the central estimate's three parameters (30 percent offset, 65 percent tax-financed, at most 80 percent on SDPs)

* **Effect.** Strengthens. It shows that the central estimate behind the headline range rests on stated assumptions about how states behave, which fits the article's point that state decisions are the main uncertainty.
* **The fix changes how this finding can be used.** After the sec. 2 and sec. 3 corrections, readers will learn that CMS's central estimate already assumes states replace 30 percent of the provider tax rule's cuts with other revenue (91 FR 46591). Two passages then need to read consistently with that:
  * The Executive summary says states have not decided how they will fund SDPs, next to a range that assumes a particular state response.
  * In sec. 7's third case, the state replaces lost SDPs with general revenue.

  One cross-reference in sec. 4 should say that the headline range is conditional on CMS's state-offset assumption as well as on step 3. Then the sec. 7 case reads as a state departing from CMS's national assumption, not as relief on top of it. Without that cross-reference, a hospital comparing its own estimate with the headline range could count state backfill twice.
* **Do not cite THA for the scenario labels.** THA calls 20 percent the low scenario, but CMS gives 20 percent for the high scenario (91 FR 46593).
* **Headline, takeaway, call to action.** The headline numbers are unchanged, but their basis gets a qualifier. The call to action gets stronger, because whether the state will backfill becomes a concrete question to ask it.
* **Crowds out.** No.
* **Placement.** **Support** in sec. 3 (the "Provider tax rule" bullet) and sec. 2 paragraph 5, as corrected, plus the one cross-reference in sec. 4 paragraph 1. Scenario detail goes in Appendix D.

### F-015: CBPP's federal comparison (corrected)

* **Effect.** Contradicts a sub-claim, not the thesis. As corrected, CMS's own tables show that adjusting for the window, the interaction and the dollar basis does not bring CMS's combined federal figure down toward CBO's:
  * Dropping 2035 leaves $501.5 billion.
  * Removing the interaction raises the total to $755.9 billion.
  * The dollar-basis adjustment is known in direction only.

  So in sec. 5's last POLITICO paragraph, the differences in years and dollar values cannot carry the weight of explaining the gap. The corrected fact also holds: CBPP's footnote says two of its three adjustments raise CMS's figure, and only the window lowers it.
* **Hold.** CBO's $340.5 billion is unverifiable (cbo.gov returned HTTP 403). So is any statement of how large a gap remains, including CBPP's conclusion. **The fix changes how this finding can be used:** the F-015 row in `required_corrections_v16.md` sets $501.5 billion against CBO's $340.5 billion.   * POLITICO's construction mixes bases, gross and net figures, and all providers with hospitals.
  * The CMS–CBO difference underneath it is not explained by the differences v16 names.
  * Its cause is unresolved, which is the ledger's own conclusion at SB-23.

  v16's [I] conclusion that the gap does not show the rules exceed the OBBBA is still logically correct, but it reads as a verdict. The note takes no position, so it should not appear to settle that question in either direction.
* **Crowds out.** Yes, as lead. It would turn sec. 5 into a contest between CMS and CBO and draw readers to whether the rules exceed the statute, a question the note disclaims. That would come at the expense of the net provider loss, which is the note's own contribution.
* **Placement.** **Support** for the CMS-table arithmetic only, in sec. 5's POLITICO and CBO subsection (last paragraph) and in Appendix F. Cite CMS (Table 19, 91 FR 30452; Table 14, 91 FR 46593; Table 11, 91 FR 46591), not CBPP. **Hold** CBPP's comparative conclusion and any figure for the remaining gap. The analyst proposed lead. This review does not adopt it, because the comparative part is unverifiable and lead placement would move the note's center.

## Verdict

**The thesis holds. It needs qualifying, not reframing.** Once the known fixes land, three qualifications follow from the findings:

1. **Hospitals (F-012).** That most of the cut falls on hospitals is CMS's expectation, not an estimate: CMS says on the same page that it cannot attribute impacts to provider types. The headline is about providers and survives. The qualification also strengthens the call to action, because a hospital's own records become the only route to a hospital figure.
2. **The range's basis (F-007).** The $488–753 billion range is conditional on CMS's stated assumptions about state behavior, including a 30 percent state offset, as well as on the step 3 unknown. One cross-reference keeps a hospital from counting state backfill twice.
3. **CMS against CBO (F-015, F-005).** Sec. 5 should keep its critique of POLITICO's construction. It should stop implying that the differences it names explain the CMS–CBO gap. On CMS's own tables they do not, and the CBO side cannot be checked in this pass. "Unresolved" is the supportable word, and it is consistent with the no-position line.

**The one change that would help the reader most:** open sec. 4 with CMS's own statement that the net effect of both rules is still a significant reduction in provider payments (F-011, 91 FR 46593), placed beside the +$21.7 billion, and point to it from Executive summary paragraph 2. The biggest risk to the reader is taking +$21.7 billion or $220.3 billion as the answer, and F-010 and F-013 show that commenters already do. CMS's sentence closes that gap. It also turns "THEIA Services found no published estimate" from THEIA's assertion into a gap CMS itself leaves open: CMS says the combined effect is a significant net reduction and publishes no figure for it.
