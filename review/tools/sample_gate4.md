# 1. The OBBBA and the two proposed rules

The OBBBA, formally Public Law 119-21 and called the Working Families Tax Cut legislation by CMS (91 FR 46562), sets two limits on how states fund Medicaid; CMS has proposed one rule for each, and both rules add provisions the OBBBA does not require. [E] A third, Section 71117, is carried out by a final rule (CMS-2448-F) that CMS’s provider tax estimates take into account and that CMS says does not significantly change them (91 FR 46590 n. 23, 46592; Appendix C). [E]

## The SDP rule (CMS-2449-P)

| **Required by the OBBBA (Section 71116)**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | **Added by CMS in the proposed rule**                                                                                        |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Caps SDPs for inpatient hospital, outpatient hospital, nursing facility and academic medical center practitioner services at 100 percent of Medicare rates in states that expanded Medicaid to low-income adults (expansion states; Appendix B) and 110 percent elsewhere. | Extends the cap to SDPs for other services: CMS estimates −$5.34 billion in spending over 2029–2035 (91 FR 30453). |
| Lets SDPs approved, or sought through a documented good-faith effort, before May 1, 2025 (by July 4, 2025 for rural hospitals) continue above the cap, then cuts these grandfathered payments by 10 percentage points a year, starting with each state’s first managed care rating period (the period for which a state sets its plans’ payment rates) on or after January 1, 2028. | Limits fee-for-service payments that target particular practitioners: CMS estimates −$2.44 billion in spending over 2029–2035 (Table 27, 91 FR 30456). |

*Source: 91 FR 30400–30456. CMS’s additions include the two shown; THEIA Services did not confirm that no others exist. Both are federal plus state spending in 2026 dollars (91 FR 30453, 30456).*

# 2. What links the two rules

Because provider taxes fund many SDPs and both rules reach tax-funded SDPs, CMS’s estimates overlap. [I] Under CMS’s central estimate, the provider tax rule would cut provider payments by $384.0 billion alone but by only $142.1 billion more once the SDP rule’s cuts are counted, and CMS attributes the $241.9 billion difference ($154.9 billion federal, $87.0 billion state) to cuts already counted in the SDP rule (91 FR 46592). [E] Any total that adds the two estimates without removing the $241.9 billion overlap counts $241.9 billion twice. [I]
